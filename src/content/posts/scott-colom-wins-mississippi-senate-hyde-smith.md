The Associated Press and NBC News project that Democrat Scott Colom has won Mississippi's U.S. Senate race, defeating Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith. [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/scott-colom-mississippi-senate-election-win-democrat-hyde-smith-rcna605729) published its projection at 5:59 p.m. ET on Wednesday, November 11 (4:59 p.m. CT). AP published a [race-call article](https://apnews.com/article/race-call-colom-wins-mississippi-u-s-senate-1787bf4967fd46cfbed2fe4d777a5781) and a [follow-up](https://apnews.com/article/scott-colom-mississippi-senate-midterms-56c18f292a734443992f9466c3b0f346) headlined, "Mississippi elects Democrat Scott Colom as state's first Black US senator since Reconstruction." [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/12/g-s1-153171/up-first-newsletter-scott-colom-trump-democrats-ukraine-russia) also reported the result on November 12.

Hyde-Smith congratulated Colom in a video message Wednesday evening, [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/scott-colom-mississippi-senate-election-win-democrat-hyde-smith-rcna605729) reported. "While this is not the outcome we worked for or hoped for, I respect the decision of Mississippi voters," she said. "I want to congratulate Scott Colom and wish him well as he takes on the tremendous responsibility of representing our great state." Colom's campaign told NBC that Hyde-Smith had called him to concede.

## What Colom said

Colom, a district attorney, spoke to supporters in Jackson on Wednesday night, NBC reported. "For months and months, I've been telling everybody, everywhere I go, that we're going to win this election," he said. "All I did was have the courage to imagine a night like this. I had the courage to believe in Mississippi." He said he would represent "the ones that voted for me, the ones that didn't vote for me and the ones that didn't vote."

## What the result would mean

NBC News reports that Mississippi last elected a Democrat to the Senate in 1982, and that Colom will become the state's first Black U.S. senator since Reconstruction. AP's headline on the result uses the same Reconstruction framing. Colom, in his remarks, said the first Black senator ever elected in the United States came from Mississippi, NBC reported.

NBC says Colom's victory means Democrats will hold at least 55 Senate seats next year, and that the only Senate race still uncalled is Alaska. NPR likewise reported that the win expands the Democratic majority in the new Congress to 55 seats, with Alaska still undecided.

## Official count still pending

These projections are not the same as a certified result. In a [November 4 statement](https://www.sos.ms.gov/news/official-statement-election-update), Mississippi Secretary of State's office said counties have 10 days from Election Day to submit certified results, "which is November 13." The office's [election results page](https://www.sos.ms.gov/elections-voting/election-results) had not posted 2026 general-election certified totals as of this writing. Unofficial statewide vote totals were still being updated by news organizations as counties finished their work, and this article does not treat any unofficial tally as final.
