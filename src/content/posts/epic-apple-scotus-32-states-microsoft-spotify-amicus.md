Thirty-two states, the District of Columbia, Microsoft, Spotify, and Match Group have filed Supreme Court friend-of-the-court briefs supporting Epic Games in *Apple Inc. v. Epic Games, Inc.*, No. 25-1311. The [Supreme Court docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-1311.html) records the state brief and the joint developer brief as filed on November 20, 2026. [9to5Mac reported the filings](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/25/major-developers-legal-experts-and-32-states-back-epic-in-apples-scotus-appeal/) on November 25.

The Court has set oral argument for **Monday, January 11, 2027**. Apple is the petitioner. The case is Apple's appeal of a civil contempt ruling from the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, which followed a district court finding that Apple violated an earlier injunction on App Store anti-steering rules. The docket shows the petition was granted limited to Question 1 presented by the petition.

## What the state brief says

Utah led the [state brief](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-1311/433509/20261120173125850_25-1311%20Amicus%20Brief.pdf), joined by 31 other states and the District of Columbia. The other states listed on the brief are Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Florida, Illinois, Indiana, Kansas, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Nebraska, Nevada, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Tennessee, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, and West Virginia.

The states write that "the district court correctly held Apple in contempt for continuing to engage in anticompetitive conduct barred by a prior injunction." They argue that after the district court required Apple to lift unlawful anti-steering restrictions, "Apple immediately tried to sidestep the injunction by tweaking its policies to ensure that developers would still not be able to steer users elsewhere." The brief also says Apple "actively hid its injunction evasion in a series of bad-faith actions culminating with an 'outright li[e] under oath,'" quoting the lower-court record.

Those are the states' arguments, and the quoted phrase is taken from the record they cite. Apple has asked the Supreme Court to overturn the contempt finding and argues that the injunction did not forbid the commissions and other restrictions it later imposed. Apple also received supporting amicus briefs earlier in the merits stage, including from the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, the Computer & Communications Industry Association, and others listed on the docket in September. The United States filed a brief supporting neither party.

## Microsoft, Spotify, and Match Group

In a [joint brief](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-1311/433525/20261120181836890_Epic%20v.%20Apple%20-%20Amicus%20Brief%20Combined.pdf) also filed November 20, Spotify USA Inc., Match Group, Inc., and Microsoft Corporation say they "offer some of the most popular and widely downloaded mobile applications" and "were subject to the measures found unlawful in this case." The brief describes Apple's response to the injunction as including design restrictions, "such as banning visually discernable buttons and requiring developers to display needless 'scare screen' warnings," together with "a commission structure that rendered any effort to direct users to alternative platforms prohibitively expensive."

The docket lists additional amicus briefs supporting Epic from the same week, including filings by former Article III judges, the American Antitrust Institute, Public Knowledge and the Electronic Frontier Foundation, the Little Tech Association, the American Association for Justice, and others.

[9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/25/major-developers-legal-experts-and-32-states-back-epic-in-apples-scotus-appeal/) reports that the Supreme Court is considering the legal standard used to uphold the civil contempt ruling, and that the separate dispute over what commission, if any, Apple can charge on purchases made through external payment links remains in the district court. A request by Apple to stay those district-court proceedings was denied in August, according to the docket.
