Valve says you can now see the **average framerate players get in games on Steam Deck**. The company's [Steam Deck Framerate](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/593110/view/685269801333948419) post, dated November 9 (about **6:38 p.m. CT**), places the figures on each game's Steam store detail page under **Steam Deck Compatibility > Learn more**. The same data is also available in the Steam Library on **Steam Client Beta**.

The **Steam Client Beta — November 9th** notes confirm the Hardware Compatibility dialog now includes that average, and that the prompt to opt in to anonymized framerate data is now also shown in the Steam desktop client. The beta announcement's RSS pubDate is **2026-11-10 00:48:39 UTC** (about **6:48 p.m. CT on November 9**).

## What Valve says the numbers are

- The average uses data from customers who **opted in** to providing anonymized framerate data.
- It is available for games tested for Deck compatibility: **Verified, Playable, and Unsupported**.
- Valve says there are **thousands** of games with data so far, with a minimum threshold of anonymous samples before averages are shown.
- An expandable **histogram** breaks down the percentage of samples at each framerate, which Valve says can show how the average is produced and how often hitching or stuttering appears as lower-framerate columns.
- The published average and histogram are **filtered to Decks set to the default frame limit**: **90 FPS on Steam Deck OLED** and **60 FPS on Steam Deck LCD**. Valve says it does **not** account for custom in-game settings, but has found the data still consolidates around relatively narrow ranges.
- A majority of Steam Deck players have opted in, Valve says. The desktop opt-in is currently only on Steam Client Beta and will ship more widely later. The setting lives in Steam Account Settings > Data & Browsing > Anonymous Game Framerate Data.

Valve frames this as a supplement to Steam Deck Verified: a Verified badge may not match the framerate some players prefer, and an Unsupported rating for unstable performance may still be acceptable to others.

## SteamDeckHQ's critique

SteamDeckHQ's Noah Kupetsky argues, in a November 10 piece titled **"Valve's New Steam Deck Framerate Data Does More Harm Than Good,"** that a single average can be misleading. The site says quality settings are unknown, later areas of a game can run much worse than the opening, mods can raise the figure without disclosure, and menu framerates may be included. As one example, Kupetsky writes that **Phantom Blade Zero** is listed at an average of **43 FPS** even though, in the site's telling, about **65%** of the game sticks to an unstable **30 FPS**.

Those examples and the "more harm than good" conclusion are **SteamDeckHQ's reported assessment**, not an independent VetTV News measurement. A claim that frame generation inflates the official averages was **not confirmed** in Valve's post or in the SteamDeckHQ article reviewed for this draft, so it is not included here.
