The Trump administration on Wednesday, November 25, proposed new tariffs on 14 trading partners, including China, the European Union, Japan, South Korea and India. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer [determined](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural) under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 that each economy's policies on manufacturing capacity are "unreasonable and burden or restrict U.S. commerce," and proposed responsive tariffs for public comment.

The tariffs are proposals, not final. [USTR said](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural) it will take comments and hold hearings first, and Greer told CNBC he does not expect any of them to take effect "earlier than a couple months from now," [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/us-tariffs-trump-section-301.html).

## What USTR proposed

According to the [USTR announcement](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural), the proposed tariffs are "up to" these levels, with exemptions for certain goods:

- **China:** up to 20% on most goods, and up to 10% on goods listed in an annex, net of the tariff already applied under the separate forced-labor Section 301 case. USTR's [China notice](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Releases/2026/China%20FRN.pdf) says the proposal reflects the 20% rate from the October 2025 Trump-Xi arrangement. It also says USTR could consider up to 40% on all Chinese goods if ongoing engagement with China is found inadequate.
- **India:** up to 25%, or 17% if India reaches a reciprocal trade agreement with the U.S.
- **Vietnam:** up to 35%, or 20% if it reaches an agreement, according to USTR and its [Vietnam notice](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Releases/2026/Vietnam%20FRN%20Final%20Signed.pdf).
- **Singapore:** up to 20%, or 10% if it reaches an agreement or concludes other talks that address USTR's findings.
- **Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand:** up to 18% each.
- **EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan:** up to 15%, net of existing most-favored-nation rates.

Mexico and Norway were also investigated, but USTR said it has [not made determinations](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural) on them yet. Reuters [reported](https://www.reuters.com/3fec1b0a85a0/world/china/us-proposes-new-tariffs-14-trading-partners-fight-excess-factory-capacity-2026-11-25/) that the proposed China rate would be added to duties of roughly 25% imposed in Trump's first term, and that the new duties would not stack on top of tariffs under the forced-labor case or the national-security duties on steel, aluminum and autos.

## Why the administration says it is doing this

Greer said in the USTR announcement that many trading partners "have adopted industrial strategies built on targeting manufacturing, wage and labor suppression, financial and other support, non-market economy inputs, and other market distorting practices" that erode America's manufacturing base. [USTR said](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural) it opened the investigations in March and heard from nearly 140 witnesses and 1,070 written comments.

The action also comes after the Supreme Court in February struck down Trump's broad global tariffs imposed under an emergency-powers law, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/3fec1b0a85a0/world/china/us-proposes-new-tariffs-14-trading-partners-fight-excess-factory-capacity-2026-11-25/). The administration has since leaned on other trade laws, including Section 301, to rebuild its tariff leverage, according to Reuters and [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/us-tariffs-trump-section-301.html).

Greer told CNBC the new rates are "not really additive" and will look more like a "top up" on existing duties, CNBC [reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/us-tariffs-trump-section-301.html). He said China is the exception, "so it will go up some."

## How the other governments responded

- **China:** A spokesperson for China's embassy in Washington said the so-called overcapacity issue "does not really exist," [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/3fec1b0a85a0/world/china/us-proposes-new-tariffs-14-trading-partners-fight-excess-factory-capacity-2026-11-25/). On Thursday, a Chinese Commerce Ministry spokesperson called on Washington to "rectify its wrongdoing," [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/us-tariffs-hike-trade-agreement.html).
- **European Union:** Commission spokesperson Olof Gill rejected the finding that Europe is a source of overcapacity and said the EU expects any measure to respect its existing 15% all-inclusive ceiling, [according to CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/us-tariffs-hike-trade-agreement.html).
- **Singapore:** Its trade ministry said it disagreed with the findings and was "very disappointed," [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/us-tariffs-hike-trade-agreement.html).
- **Vietnam:** Its trade ministry said it is working with the U.S. to finish a trade deal quickly, [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/us-tariffs-hike-trade-agreement.html), citing Reuters.

In Washington, Sen. Ron Wyden, an Oregon Democrat expected to chair the Senate Finance Committee in January, said he will "use every tool at my disposal to end Trump's disastrous tariffs," [according to Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/3fec1b0a85a0/world/china/us-proposes-new-tariffs-14-trading-partners-fight-excess-factory-capacity-2026-11-25/).

## Also announced Wednesday

USTR separately [proposed changes](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-invites-public-comment-proposed-modifications-section-301-action-regarding-brazils-unreasonable) to its Section 301 action on Brazil, including an additional 10% tariff on Brazilian finished aircraft and a higher out-of-quota tariff on sugar. Comments on that proposal are due December 30, with a hearing on January 14, 2027.

## What happens next

Requests to appear and written comments on the 14 proposals are due [January 5, 2027](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural), and USTR will hold hearings starting [January 25, 2027](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural). Reuters [reported](https://www.reuters.com/3fec1b0a85a0/world/china/us-proposes-new-tariffs-14-trading-partners-fight-excess-factory-capacity-2026-11-25/) that the tariffs would not take effect until after that hearing. [NPR reported](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/26/g-s1-155950/up-first-newsletter-thanksgiving-weather-travel-trump-tariffs-iowa-detonation-fcc-free-speech) that for each country the White House listed a "proposed" rate and a lower "deal" rate.

## What's still unclear

USTR has not set final rates or a start date. CNBC [reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/26/us-tariffs-hike-trade-agreement.html) that a senior U.S. official said the duties could take effect as early as January, while Greer said a couple of months, so the timing is not settled. It is also unclear how much of the final tariff burden would be new once existing duties are netted out, since officials describe the rates as a "top up" while critics say they raise costs. We have not reviewed every country's annex of exempted products, and the final scope could change after the comment period. This is a developing story, and we'll update it as USTR moves forward.
