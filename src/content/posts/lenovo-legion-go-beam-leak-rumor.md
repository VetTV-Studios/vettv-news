**This is a leak, and it remains unconfirmed.** Lenovo has not announced or confirmed a device called the Legion Go Beam. The new details below come from material Windows Latest says it obtained, not from a Lenovo announcement.

**Editor's note, Nov. 25, 2026:** This article was first published on Nov. 19, after [Windows Latest](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/11/18/exclusive-lenovo-legion-go-beam-is-a-simpler-handheld-that-could-undercut-the-legion-go-2-which-now-costs-up-to-1999/) published three images of an unannounced handheld it called the Legion Go Beam and said it could not learn the operating system, the specs or the price. On Nov. 24, Windows Latest published a [follow-up](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/11/24/exclusive-lenovos-legion-go-beam-skips-windows-11-and-steamos-for-android-with-a-7-inch-120hz-screen-and-pc-game-streaming/) based on additional leaked material, saying the Beam is an Android handheld. The headline, summary and this article have been updated. Lenovo still has not confirmed the device.

## What Windows Latest now reports

On November 24, [Windows Latest](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/11/24/exclusive-lenovos-legion-go-beam-skips-windows-11-and-steamos-for-android-with-a-7-inch-120hz-screen-and-pc-game-streaming/) reported that "new details obtained" by the site describe the Legion Go Beam as a 7-inch handheld that "plays native Android games." The site says the material it obtained also lists a 120Hz display, Wi-Fi 6E and Legion Space, and that the device can "stream from your PC or console," play cloud games and run retro titles. Windows Latest writes that the Android games "run on the Beam itself," while a streamed game runs on the PC and the Beam is used "as the screen and controller."

[9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/24/lenovo-legion-go-beam-leak/) reported the same follow-up at 10:51 a.m. CT on November 24, and [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/lenovo-legion-go-beam-3738970/) followed at 1:28 p.m. CT that day. [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1042020/lenovos-leaked-go-beam-handheld-will-apparently-run-android) reported it at 3:25 a.m. CT on November 25, writing that the leaked Go Beam "will apparently run Android" and quoting Windows Latest's line that it will "play native Android titles." [VideoCardz](https://videocardz.com/newz/lenovo-legion-go-beam-reportedly-features-android-snapdragon-g2-gen-1-and-120hz-display) followed at 7:22 a.m. CT the same day.

VetTV News has not seen the leaked material or the original image files, and we could not independently verify them.

## The chip is still uncertain

Windows Latest says "the information we have at the moment points to Qualcomm's Snapdragon G2 Gen 1, though that could change before launch given the chip's age." VideoCardz similarly notes that the processor choice could still change. There is no official confirmation of a chip.

We are not repeating a secondary claim that the Beam would ship with Android 16. Windows Latest's follow-up does not say that. Its Android 16 reference is about Lenovo's Legion C700, a different handheld sold in China.

## Price and timing are still unknown

There is still no price and no confirmed launch date. Windows Latest does not list either in the follow-up. Earlier coverage treated CES 2027 as a likely venue because Lenovo often announces Legion hardware there; [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1036819/the-leaked-lenovo-legion-go-beam-could-be-the-affordable-handheld-we-need) expected an official announcement then, while cautioning that availability could come months later. [Windows Latest's original report](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/11/18/exclusive-lenovo-legion-go-beam-is-a-simpler-handheld-that-could-undercut-the-legion-go-2-which-now-costs-up-to-1999/) pointed to Lenovo's Tech World event at CES 2027 on January 6 but noted Lenovo hadn't mentioned gaming for it. Those remain predictions, not a confirmed date.

Windows Latest also says the leaked material describes "long-lasting battery life," attributed to Lenovo. No battery capacity has been reported.

## What the leaked images appeared to show

Based on [Windows Latest's original description](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/11/18/exclusive-lenovo-legion-go-beam-is-a-simpler-handheld-that-could-undercut-the-legion-go-2-which-now-costs-up-to-1999/) and images, published November 18:

- A white body with lime-green accents, including green rings around both thumbsticks and a green strip along the bottom edge.
- Built-in controllers that Windows Latest believes don't detach, which would mean no kickstand or FPS mouse mode like the original Legion Go and Legion Go 2.
- Thick screen bezels, with room for a Legion logo under the display.
- An Xbox-style stick layout: left stick above the D-pad, right stick below the face buttons.
- No touchpad below the right stick, unlike the Legion Go S.
- On the top edge: a power button, volume buttons, **one USB-C port**, a 3.5mm headphone jack, a microSD slot and two exhaust vents. Windows Latest notes the Legion Go S has two USB-C ports, but it had no image of the Beam's bottom edge, so a second port there can't be ruled out.
- Two rear buttons labeled Y1 and Y2, plus the usual shoulder buttons and triggers.

Windows Latest's follow-up argues that a single USB-C port and the missing touchpad make more sense if the device is a streaming-focused Android handheld rather than a Windows PC. That is the site's analysis, not a Lenovo explanation.

**Our take (opinion):** if the leak is real, an Android Legion handheld built around streaming and native Android games would be a different product from the Windows and SteamOS Legion Go models, and the price would decide whether it matters. Until Lenovo says something, treat every detail here as unconfirmed.
