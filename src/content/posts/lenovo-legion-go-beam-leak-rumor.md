**This is a rumor.** Lenovo has not announced or confirmed a device called the Legion Go Beam, and nothing below comes from Lenovo.

[Windows Latest](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/11/18/exclusive-lenovo-legion-go-beam-is-a-simpler-handheld-that-could-undercut-the-legion-go-2-which-now-costs-up-to-1999/) reported on November 18 that Lenovo is working on a new gaming handheld called the Legion Go Beam, and published three images it describes as exclusive. The site says it could not learn the specs, the price, or whether the device runs Windows 11 or SteamOS. It also says it has no official confirmation from Lenovo. VetTV News has not seen the device or the images' original files, and we could not independently verify them.

## What the leaked images appear to show

Based on [Windows Latest's description](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/11/18/exclusive-lenovo-legion-go-beam-is-a-simpler-handheld-that-could-undercut-the-legion-go-2-which-now-costs-up-to-1999/) and images:

- A white body with lime-green accents, including green rings around both thumbsticks and a green strip along the bottom edge.
- Built-in controllers that Windows Latest believes don't detach, which would mean no kickstand or FPS mouse mode like the original Legion Go and Legion Go 2.
- Thick screen bezels, with room for a Legion logo under the display.
- An Xbox-style stick layout: left stick above the D-pad, right stick below the face buttons.
- No touchpad below the right stick, unlike the Legion Go S.
- On the top edge: a power button, volume buttons, **one USB-C port**, a 3.5mm headphone jack, a microSD slot and two exhaust vents. Windows Latest notes the Legion Go S has two USB-C ports, but it had no image of the Beam's bottom edge, so a second port there can't be ruled out.
- Two rear buttons labeled Y1 and Y2, plus the usual shoulder buttons and triggers.

## Windows or SteamOS?

Nobody knows yet. [Windows Latest](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/11/18/exclusive-lenovo-legion-go-beam-is-a-simpler-handheld-that-could-undercut-the-legion-go-2-which-now-costs-up-to-1999/) says it could not find out. [The Verge's Sean Hollister](https://www.theverge.com/games/1036819/the-leaked-lenovo-legion-go-beam-could-be-the-affordable-handheld-we-need) noted there is no SteamOS logo where one would normally go and said the device "likely runs Windows instead," while floating the idea, as speculation, that the "Beam" name could point to a streaming- or cloud-focused handheld. [VideoCardz](https://videocardz.com/newz/lenovo-legion-go-beam-handheld-leaks-ahead-of-ces-2027) said both Windows 11 and SteamOS remain possible, since Lenovo already sells the Legion Go S with either.

## Price and timing

There is no price. Windows Latest and The Verge both read the cheaper-looking hardware as a sign Lenovo is aiming lower than its current models, which have gotten more expensive. Windows Latest says the Legion Go 2 started at $1,099.99 and has climbed to $1,499.99 and $1,999.99 at Best Buy as RAM prices rose. Hollister declined to guess a price.

On timing, [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1036819/the-leaked-lenovo-legion-go-beam-could-be-the-affordable-handheld-we-need) says Lenovo has a history of announcing gaming devices at CES in January and that it expects an official Legion Go Beam announcement there, while cautioning that availability could come months later. [Windows Latest](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/11/18/exclusive-lenovo-legion-go-beam-is-a-simpler-handheld-that-could-undercut-the-legion-go-2-which-now-costs-up-to-1999/) points to Lenovo's Tech World event at CES 2027 on January 6 but notes Lenovo hasn't mentioned gaming for it. Both are predictions, not a confirmed launch date.

**Our take (opinion):** a cheaper Legion Go would be welcome if the leak is real, but one USB-C port and no touchpad are trade-offs worth weighing once real specs and a price exist. Until Lenovo says something, treat every detail here as unconfirmed.
