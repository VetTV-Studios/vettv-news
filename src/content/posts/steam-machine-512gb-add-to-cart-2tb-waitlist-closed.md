Valve's Steam Hardware group [posted "Steam Machine: Reservation and waitlist updates"](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/steam_hardware/announcements/detail/687522235410285039) on November 19 at 12:57 p.m. CT. The short version is that the 512GB model is now a straight purchase, the 2TB waitlist is closed to new signups, and Valve is warning that rising memory and storage costs leave future supply — and future prices — unclear.

"We've been able to fulfill all current reservations for the 512GB model with no waitlist remaining, and have wrapped up sending purchase invites out to all remaining 2TB reservation holders," Valve wrote.

## 512GB: buy it while supplies last

On the 512GB model, Valve is direct: "Steam Machine 512GB is now in an add-to-cart status, and you can purchase one directly while supplies last." If that stock runs out, the company says it will not start the line over. "If and when we get through current inventory, we will flip it to out-of-stock rather than begin building another waitlist."

When [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1038010/valve-steam-machine-waitlist-supply) checked Valve's website on November 19, the 512GB versions with and without a bundled controller were showing estimated delivery of three to five business days. We did not independently reconfirm that delivery window on the morning of November 20.

## 2TB: the waitlist is closed to new names

The 2TB model is tighter. Valve says it has "finished getting purchase invites out to all reservation holders." People already on the waitlist "will retain their position in line," but "as always, no one on the waitlist is guaranteed a unit."

"Because there are more people on the waitlist than units available, we are closing the waitlist to additional signups," Valve said. "The language on the product page will change from 'add to waitlist' to 'out of stock.'" Valve told anyone on that list, "you are welcome to choose to purchase a 512GB model instead (while supplies last)," with the caveat that "doing so requires leaving your spot on the waitlist." Valve "expect[s] to have more details to share in January about additional supply."

## The price warning, in Valve's words

Valve is still building more units, but it is not promising the next batch will cost the same, or that it can say how large that batch will be:

"We are continuing to build more Steam Machine units, but memory and storage costs continue to increase at unprecedented levels; as a result we don't yet know the number we are able to make, and at what price we'll be able to offer them. The 2TB model in particular will be especially constrained."

That is a warning, not a new price. Valve has not announced a hike. [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/hardware/steam-machines/valve-may-raise-steam-machine-prices-yet-again-memory-and-storage-costs-continue-to-increase-at-unprecedented-levels/) read the same post as a sign prices may rise; the only figure Valve itself used is that it does not yet know the price.

## What the US store listed on November 20

On the morning of November 20, at 8:03 a.m. CT, [Valve's US Steam Machine store catalog](https://store.steampowered.com/hardware/steammachine) listed these prices, with no discount:

- Steam Machine 512GB: **$1,049.00**
- Steam Machine 512GB with Controller: **$1,128.00**
- Steam Machine 2TB: **$1,349.00**
- Steam Machine 2TB with Controller: **$1,428.00**

Those are the listed prices, not a confirmation that every configuration is in stock. Availability is the part to take from Valve's November 19 post: 512GB is add-to-cart while supplies last; the 2TB waitlist is closed to new signups. Valve says it anticipates having more information in January.
