President Donald Trump has ordered every company that holds a federal contract to tell the White House when Congress asks about its government work, and to follow White House instructions on what to hold back.

[Executive Order 14438](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/11/protecting-the-executives-privileges-and-enhancing-efficiencies-in-federal-contracting/), titled "Protecting the Executive's Privileges and Enhancing Efficiencies in Federal Contracting," is dated Friday, November 20, and was posted on the White House website. [CNBC, citing Reuters](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/21/trump-orders-federal-contractors-to-notify-white-house-before-giving-congress-information.html), and [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/trump-orders-federal-contractors-alert-white-house-congressional-record-requests) reported that Trump signed it late Friday. It arrives as Democrats, who won majorities in both chambers in this month's midterm elections, prepare to take control of Congress in January with promises of wide-ranging investigations into the administration.

## What the order requires

According to the [text of the order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/11/protecting-the-executives-privileges-and-enhancing-efficiencies-in-federal-contracting/), all federal contractors must "immediately":

- Notify the White House and their contracting agencies of "any pending or future congressional request" about their communications or work with the executive branch.
- Follow any instructions from the White House or the contracting agency about withholding communications that the White House or agency has decided are protected by executive privilege.
- Make sure their subcontractors on federal contracts follow the same rules. The order applies to subcontractors too.

Agencies must tell their contractors about the new obligations and coordinate with the White House on how to answer congressional requests, including when to assert privilege. The Office of Management and Budget is told to write guidance on checking compliance.

For contractors that do not comply, the order says agencies "shall consider" taking action "consistent with applicable law," including terminating or canceling contracts and debarring or suspending the companies from future federal work.

## The White House's case

The order argues that executive privilege, which it describes as rooted in the Constitution's separation of powers, protects confidential executive branch information, including information shared with contractors. It says Congress cannot seek information from agencies, employees or contractors "without a valid legislative purpose that does not intrude on core Executive functions," and cannot obtain privileged material before the executive branch has a chance to assert privilege.

The order accuses "certain congressional leaders" of threatening to intrude on those powers by "harassing private companies that contract with the Federal Government." It also makes an efficiency argument: if contractors answer Congress on their own, the government may have to go to court separately to defend its privileges, and agencies may stop sharing sensitive information, including national security work, with contractors at all.

According to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/21/trump-orders-federal-contractors-to-notify-white-house-before-giving-congress-information.html), the White House defended the order as necessary to protect sensitive government communications and said contractors answering Congress on their own could expose privileged information and pull the administration into costly legal fights.

## Part of a wider push before January

The order follows a one-page memo White House Counsel Will Scharf sent to agency lawyers on Monday, November 16, [published in full by PBS News](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/read-the-full-white-house-memo-requiring-agencies-to-get-approval-before-testifying-in-front-of-congress) and first reported by [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/16/exclusive-white-house-requires-signoff-before-agency-staffers-testify-to-congress). It requires agencies to get approval from the White House Counsel's Office and the Office of Legislative Affairs "before any agency personnel may agree to appear before any congressional committee."

A White House official told [PBS News](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/read-the-full-white-house-memo-requiring-agencies-to-get-approval-before-testifying-in-front-of-congress): "The Trump Administration will not be extorted, intimidated, or threatened into submission by politically motivated baseless investigations. We are ready to engage with Congress."

Earlier, in a letter to Rep. Robert Garcia of California, the top Democrat on the House Oversight Committee, Scharf wrote that Democrats' promised investigations "may cross the line into blatant criminality," [NPR reported](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/12/nx-s1-6037540/trump-white-house-democrats-investigations-robert-garcia).

## What Democrats have said

Garcia is expected to chair the Oversight Committee in January. [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/21/trump-orders-federal-contractors-to-notify-white-house-before-giving-congress-information.html) that the offices of Garcia, House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer and Sen. Elizabeth Warren did not respond to requests for comment Friday night.

Garcia has answered the White House's earlier moves. After Scharf's letter, he said in a [Nov. 11 statement](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/ranking-member-robert-garcia-statement-on-new-white-house-counsel-threats): "Congress' authority comes from Article I of the Constitution, not from the permission of the White House." He told [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/12/nx-s1-6037540/trump-white-house-democrats-investigations-robert-garcia): "We're not going to be deterred, and we're certainly not going to be intimidated by Donald Trump or anyone in the administration."

On Wednesday, November 18, Garcia [sent 12 document preservation notices](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/incoming-oversight-chairman-robert-garcia-launches-document-preservation-letters-on-trump-family-corruption-corporate-corruption-dhs-misconduct-and-epstein-cover-up) to people including Vice President JD Vance, White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller, Attorney General Todd Blanche, Donald Trump Jr., Eric Trump and Jared Kushner. "These investigations will lay the groundwork for legislation which helps all Americans. This is just the beginning," Garcia said.

VetTV News has covered the incoming Democratic House leadership, including [Jeffries' nomination for speaker](/posts/house-democrats-nominate-jeffries-speaker-251-11/), as well as [Miller's appointment as chief of staff](/posts/trump-names-stephen-miller-chief-of-staff-replacing-susie-wiles/).

## Legal questions ahead

The order leans on a standard the Supreme Court spelled out in [Trump v. Mazars](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/19-715_febh.pdf) in 2020: a congressional subpoena must serve a "valid legislative purpose" and cannot be used for law enforcement. That ruling also told courts to weigh separation-of-powers concerns when Congress subpoenas a president's personal information.

[CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/21/trump-orders-federal-contractors-to-notify-white-house-before-giving-congress-information.html) and [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/trump-orders-federal-contractors-alert-white-house-congressional-record-requests) noted that the order could face legal challenges, especially if contractors are told to withhold records Congress demands by subpoena. Executive privilege lets presidents protect some confidential communications, but courts can step in when the branches clash over Congress' power to get information.

The order does not say how contractors should respond if they are subpoenaed and the White House tells them to withhold material. It also says it creates no legal right that anyone can enforce against the government.
