Epic's official Launcher Preview site lists **Ubuntu 22.04 LTS or newer** and **Debian 12 or newer** as supported Linux distributions. It says **Fedora support is coming soon**, while other distributions may work but are not officially tested or supported.

The site documents installation with an **EpicGamesLauncher-x86_64.deb** package. That establishes the current published Debian/Ubuntu packaging route, not a finished multi-distribution rollout.

## What Jake Jones told GamingOnLinux

GamingOnLinux's Liam Squires-Hand reports a statement from **Jake Jones, Communications Director at Epic Games**:

“Hey Liam! The new Epic Games Launcher is still in Preview and we plan to support as many Linux distributions as possible, including Fedora, SteamOS, Cachy, Omarchy, and more. We’re testing and evaluating packaging formats for distribution, and we’ll restore AppImage this month.”

That quotation is reproduced from **GamingOnLinux's reporting**, not a separate Epic transcript obtained by VetTV News. The article was published November 6 and updated November 7, making “this month” November 2026. The planned AppImage return is not presented as an already available download.

The stated ambition to support SteamOS and other distributions should not be confused with the narrower support list currently published in Epic's FAQ.

## Native launcher does not mean every game is native

Epic's FAQ says the launcher runs natively on Linux, but many games remain Windows-only and may require a compatibility layer. It advises checking each game's compatibility. A launcher installer alone does not establish compatibility for every title in a library.

The Preview remains unfinished: Epic warns some features may not work as expected. No Flatpak release is established by these sources; GamingOnLinux's Flatpak suggestion is commentary rather than an Epic commitment.
