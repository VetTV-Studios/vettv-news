The short answer is no, not directly. Grand Theft Auto VI has no version for Mac, iPhone or iPad, and the one cloud option Microsoft announced for launch day doesn't reach Apple devices. If you own a PlayStation 5 or an Xbox Series X|S, though, there is an official way to get the game onto an Apple screen: Remote Play.

**VetTV News has not tested streaming GTA VI to a Mac, iPhone or iPad.** What follows comes from Rockstar's, Sony's, Microsoft's and Apple's own pages, checked on the morning of November 19.

## Where GTA VI actually runs

Rockstar's launch-day release, [published by Take-Two](https://taketwointeractivesoftwareinc.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/rockstar-games-announces-grand-theft-auto-vi-now-available) on November 19, says the game "is now available for the PlayStation® 5 computer entertainment systems and the Xbox Series X|S games and entertainment system." It names no other platform. As we reported when it [went live overnight](/posts/gta-6-out-now-ps5-xbox-series-launch/), the U.S. unlock was 11:00 p.m. CT on November 18, and no official PC version or PC date has been announced.

## Why Xbox Cloud Gaming doesn't help on Apple devices

Xbox Cloud Gaming normally runs in a web browser on iPhone, iPad and Mac. Microsoft's [supported-browsers page](https://support.xbox.com/en-US/help/games-apps/cloud-gaming/supported-browsers) lists iOS 14.4, iPadOS 14.4 and macOS 14.1.2 or later for cloud play at xbox.com/play. GTA VI is announced differently.

[Xbox Wire's November 10 post](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/11/10/grand-theft-auto-6-comes-to-xbox-cloud-gaming-november-19/) says the game "will be playable on day one through Xbox Cloud Gaming on Xbox One and Xbox Series X|S, starting at 1am ET," which was midnight CT on November 19. [Rockstar's November 10 Newswire post](https://www.rockstargames.com/newswire/article/89834o94713o21/pre-load-grand-theft-auto-vi-now) uses the same wording: the game is "available via XBOX Cloud Gaming on XBOX Series X|S and XBOX One consoles, utilizing XBOX Series S graphics settings." Neither post names phones, tablets, browsers or computers. [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/xbox/grand-theft-auto-6-will-be-playable-on-xbox-cloud-gaming-but-there-are-a-few-catches) reported the same day that the cloud version "is only limited to Xbox consoles" and that you "can't stream it on PC or mobile devices via the cloud."

Neither Microsoft nor Rockstar has said why, or whether that will change. Xbox Wire also notes that the game "must be purchased separately" for cloud play.

## The official route: Remote Play from your own console

Remote Play is different from cloud gaming. The game runs on a console in your home, and your Apple device shows the picture and sends back your controller inputs. Rockstar's [Newswire post](https://www.rockstargames.com/newswire/article/89834o94713o21/pre-load-grand-theft-auto-vi-now) says: "GTAVI will also fully support Remote Play on PlayStation and XBOX."

You need three things: a PS5 or an Xbox Series X|S, your own copy of GTA VI installed on it, and a fast, steady connection at both ends.

### From a PS5: Sony's PS Remote Play app

Sony makes PS Remote Play for all three Apple platforms.

- **iPhone and iPad:** The [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ps-remote-play/id1436192460) says the free app requires iOS 16.0 or iPadOS 16.0 or later. It supports the DualSense, the DUALSHOCK 4 and, on iOS or iPadOS 16.4 or later, the DualSense Edge, plus the Backbone One PlayStation Edition on iPhone. You can also use on-screen touch controls.
- **Mac:** Sony offers a direct download, not a Mac App Store app. Its [Mac setup page](https://remoteplay.dl.playstation.net/remoteplay/lang/en/1100002.html) lists macOS Monterey, Ventura, Sonoma, Sequoia and Tahoe, with 40 MB of storage and 2 GB of RAM. Sony's [update notes](https://remoteplay.dl.playstation.net/remoteplay/lang/en/index.html) say version 9.5.0 for Mac dropped macOS Big Sur. A DualSense connects over Bluetooth or USB, and Sony says you get haptic feedback over USB. The DualSense Edge pairs over Bluetooth on macOS Ventura 13.3 or later.
- **Speed:** Sony recommends at least 5 Mbps up and down, and at least 15 Mbps for best performance, according to its [Mac](https://remoteplay.dl.playstation.net/remoteplay/lang/en/1100002.html) and [mobile](https://remoteplay.dl.playstation.net/remoteplay/lang/en/1100003.html) setup pages. The mobile page warns that Remote Play "uses a lot more data than most video streaming services."
- **On the PS5:** Go to Settings > System > Remote Play and turn on Enable Remote Play. To wake the console from rest mode, turn on Stay Connected to the Internet and Enable Turning On PS5 from Network under Settings > System > Power Saving > Features Available in Rest Mode. Sign in to the app with the same PlayStation account as the console.

### From an Xbox Series X|S: Xbox remote play in a browser

Microsoft's route runs in a browser rather than through an App Store app. The [Xbox Remote Play page](https://www.xbox.com/en-US/consoles/remote-play) tells you to go to xbox.com/remoteplay. It lists an Xbox Series X|S or Xbox One with remote features turned on, a Microsoft account with an Xbox profile, and 5 GHz Wi-Fi or a mobile data connection with at least 10 Mbps down. The [setup guide](https://support.xbox.com/en-US/help/games-apps/game-setup-and-play/how-to-set-up-remote-play) starts with turning on the Sleep power option and remote features on the console.

Microsoft's pages don't agree on which Apple devices count. The [Remote Play page](https://www.xbox.com/en-US/consoles/remote-play) lists iOS 15.1 or later and doesn't mention macOS. The [supported-browsers page](https://support.xbox.com/en-US/help/games-apps/cloud-gaming/supported-browsers), which covers both xbox.com/play and xbox.com/remoteplay, lists iOS and iPadOS 14.4 or later in Safari 14 or Chrome 85, and macOS 14.1.2 or later in Safari 16, Edge or Chrome 85. It also says mouse and keyboard work on Mac and PC in Edge or Chrome. The [Xbox app's App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/xbox/id736179781) describes social, store and remote-install features but doesn't list remote play among them. iPhone and iPad are on both of Microsoft's lists. Mac is only on the browser list, so treat Mac as less certain.

One catch for Xbox One owners: Xbox Remote Play streams games installed on your console, and GTA VI doesn't run natively on Xbox One. **Our reading:** an Xbox One can't be the source for this route, so you need a Series X or Series S.

### Controllers

GTA VI is a controller game. Apple's [controller guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111099) covers pairing Xbox, PlayStation and other Bluetooth controllers with iPhone, iPad and Mac. Put the controller in pairing mode, then pick it in Bluetooth settings. Sony's app supports touch controls, and the Xbox page mentions mobile touch controls, but neither Sony nor Microsoft says whether GTA VI has its own touch layout. Plan on a controller.

## What about Veyra?

Veyra, the free app we covered in [our how-to](/posts/veyra-ps5-remote-play-pc-upscaling-frame-gen-how-to/), adds PC upscaling and frame generation on top of PS5 Remote Play. **It runs only on Windows 11, so it is no help on a Mac, iPhone or iPad.** It is also unofficial software that we have not tested.

## Opinion: set your expectations

*This section is opinion.* Remote Play is a decent way to play in another room or while traveling, as long as you already own the console. It is not a substitute for playing on the console itself. Your picture depends on your home connection's upload speed, and fast driving and gunfights will show any lag or compression first. If you can, connect the console to your router with a cable, use 5 GHz Wi-Fi on the Apple side, and expect a softer image than on your TV.
