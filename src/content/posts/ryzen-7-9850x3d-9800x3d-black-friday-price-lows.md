AMD's two fastest eight-core gaming processors are on sale heading into Black Friday, but the prices are not the same at every store, and some of the lowest online figures only apply after a coupon or promo code. **Prices change quickly.** I rechecked major U.S. listings on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, around 10 a.m. CT, and attributed earlier figures to the outlets that reported them.

The sharpest verified prices today are at [Micro Center](https://www.microcenter.com/product/706001/amd-ryzen-7-9850x3d-granite-ridge-am5-470ghz-8-core-boxed-processor-heatsink-not-included), which is advertising the [Ryzen 7 9850X3D](https://www.amd.com/en/products/processors/desktops/ryzen/9000-series/amd-ryzen-7-9850x3d.html) at **$399.99** in-store, $100 off its $499.99 listed price, and the [Ryzen 7 9800X3D](https://www.microcenter.com/product/687907/amd-ryzen-7-9800x3d-granite-ridge-am5-470ghz-8-core-boxed-processor-heatsink-not-included) at **$349.99** in-store, $130 off its $479.99 listed price. Both deals are in-store pickup only, with no shipping, and are limited to one per household. Availability varies by store.

That $349.99 9800X3D price is $30 lower than the $379.99 in-store figure [AMD itself listed](https://www.amd.com/en/blogs/2026/are-there-any-good-gaming-deals-right-now.html) after checking Micro Center on November 23, and lower than the $379.99 [Wccftech reported](https://wccftech.com/amd-ryzen-desktop-cpus-are-now-up-to-200-off-as-holiday-season-approaches/) on November 19.

## Official specs and launch prices

Both chips are eight-core, 16-thread Zen 5 desktop processors for AMD's AM5 socket, with 96MB of L3 cache, a 120W default TDP, and second-generation 3D V-Cache. AMD's product pages list a 4.7 GHz base clock on both. The 9850X3D boosts up to **5.6 GHz**; the 9800X3D boosts up to **5.2 GHz**. Neither boxed processor includes a cooler.

Those specs come from [AMD's Ryzen 7 9850X3D page](https://www.amd.com/en/products/processors/desktops/ryzen/9000-series/amd-ryzen-7-9850x3d.html) and [Ryzen 7 9800X3D page](https://www.amd.com/en/products/processors/desktops/ryzen/9000-series/amd-ryzen-7-9800x3d.html). The 9850X3D launched on January 29, 2026. The 9800X3D launched on November 7, 2024.

AMD's official suggested prices are **$499** for the 9850X3D and **$479** for the 9800X3D, not the $499.99 and $479.99 figures some deal writeups use. AMD's U.S. shop still shows those suggested prices as the prior "was" amounts: the 9850X3D is listed at [$454](https://shop-us-en.amd.com/amd-ryzen-7-9850x3d-processor/), down from $499, and the 9800X3D at [$429](https://shop-us-en.amd.com/amd-ryzen-7-9800x3d-processor/), down from $479, as of November 24. AMD's October 31, 2024 [9800X3D launch announcement](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-10-31-the-gaming-legend-continues--amd-introduces-next-.html) set the 9800X3D's suggested price at $479. The $499 9850X3D price was confirmed ahead of that chip's January 29 launch, including in [The Verge's report](https://www.theverge.com/news/865648/amd-ryzen-7-9850x3d-price-release-date-announcement) and [VideoCardz's account](https://videocardz.com/newz/amd-confirms-ryzen-7-9850x3d-launches-january-29-at-499) of AMD's confirmation. Some retailers, including Micro Center, still advertise against $499.99 and $479.99 list prices.

## What the two chips actually differ by

The 9850X3D is a higher-clocked version of the 9800X3D, not a new core layout. In its late-2026 CPU guide, published November 24, [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/bestof/cpu-26-27/) said Amazon had brought the 9850X3D to $399, matching the 9800X3D, and that the newer chip is "about 5% faster on average." The same piece called that 4% to 5% gain "not exactly exciting" and noted that, in TechSpot's original review, the extra performance comes with substantially higher power use. At identical prices, TechSpot said it would take the extra performance, and that there is "really no reason to buy the 9800X3D at the same price."

That 4% to 5% range matches TechSpot's [9850X3D review](https://www.techspot.com/review/3082-amd-ryzen-9850x3d/), which found the newer chip 5% faster than the 9800X3D on a 14-game geomean and about 4% faster on a per-game average at Medium settings. [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/amd-ryzen-7-9850x3d-vs-ryzen-7-9800x3d), in a head-to-head, measured a smaller 3.2% average gaming lead at 1080p and reported much higher power draw in games, 106W versus 81.4W. Tom's also found that enabling Precision Boost Overdrive on the 9800X3D can close much of the gap. I am citing those reviews, not repeating a new test of my own.

## Our take

This is opinion. If you can get to a Micro Center, the $349.99 9800X3D is the standout in today's listings: $50 less than the in-store 9850X3D, with only a small, well-documented gaming difference. The "buy the 9850X3D when the two cost the same" advice from TechSpot still makes sense on the Amazon coupon path, if both coupons actually apply to your account and bring the chips to about $399. Newegg's own 9850X3D listing is not in that $399 to $419 range today. Check the final checkout price, the seller, and whether you already have a cooler, because neither boxed chip includes one.
