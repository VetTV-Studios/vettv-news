Measles is no longer considered eliminated in the United States. The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) said Tuesday, November 17, that endemic measles transmission has been re-established in the United States, Mexico and Bolivia, and that all three countries have therefore lost their measles elimination status. PAHO set out the decision in a [news release](https://www.paho.org/en/news/17-11-2026-paho-urges-countries-step-efforts-stop-measles-outbreaks-and-move-towards) and a [questions-and-answers document](https://www.paho.org/en/news/17-11-2026-measles-americas-questions-and-answers-technical-reference-journalists-and-public) posted the same day.

The U.S. has held that status since 2000, according to the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/data-research/index.html). Losing it means the virus has once again set up sustained local spread in the country. It does not mean every part of the country has an outbreak.

## What PAHO found

PAHO's Measles, Rubella and Congenital Rubella Syndrome Elimination Regional Monitoring and Re-Verification Commission, an independent technical panel, reviewed epidemiological and laboratory evidence from all 47 reporting countries and territories in the Americas at its sixth annual meeting, held November 10 to 13. For the United States, Mexico and Bolivia, PAHO said in the Q&A, the commission "concluded that all three countries had lost their verification status as free from endemic measles transmission, as the virus had circulated continuously in each country for at least 12 months."

PAHO Director Dr. Jarbas Barbosa "received and agreed with the Commission's report," the organization said, and told each country's health authority about the change. In Tuesday's news release, Barbosa said: "Losing measles elimination status is much more than losing a public health designation. It means the virus has circulated for a prolonged period, putting families and communities at risk of preventable illness, hospitalization and death."

PAHO's timeline for the U.S. is specific. The outbreak it counted began on January 20, 2025, and passed the 12-month mark on January 20, 2026. That matches the analysis window PAHO announced in [March](https://www.paho.org/en/news/2-3-2026-update-review-measles-elimination-status), when it set the U.S. and Mexico reviews for this November meeting. For Mexico, the clock started February 1, 2025. For Bolivia, it started April 16, 2025.

Under the commission's criteria, endemic transmission is considered re-established when the same measles virus genotype and lineage circulate uninterrupted for 12 months or more in a country. PAHO's public documents do not say which U.S. transmission chain the commission judged continuous. A senior Trump administration official told [Reuters](https://gvwire.com/2026/11/16/us-loses-measles-free-status-after-26-year-run-source-says/) on Monday that PAHO informed the administration in a letter on Friday, and that the Utah outbreak, which began in June 2025, was key. VetTV News has not seen that letter. The commission is expected to publish a final report later, according to PAHO's [October announcement](https://www.paho.org/en/news/14-10-2026-measles-and-rubella-elimination-regional-monitoring-and-re-verification-commission) of the meeting.

## The numbers

- **U.S. cases:** The CDC, in a page updated November 13, says 5,412 confirmed measles cases were reported in 2026 as of November 12, including 5,394 from 47 jurisdictions and 18 among international visitors. That is up from 2,289 for all of 2025. VetTV News [reported](/posts/us-measles-cases-top-5000-highest-since-1991/) on November 8 that the count had passed 5,000, then the highest annual total since 1991.
- **Outbreaks:** The CDC counts 43 new outbreaks in 2026, and 96% of this year's cases (5,216 of 5,412) are outbreak-associated.
- **Deaths:** The CDC lists six measles deaths in 2026 and says that number may change. [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/public-health/us-measles-elimination-status-lost-outbreaks-continuous-spread-year-paho-cdc/) says states have reported 13 measles-associated deaths across 2025 and 2026.
- **The region:** PAHO's Tuesday news release says the Americas had reported 58,213 confirmed measles cases and 82 deaths as of November 13, compared with 12,806 cases during the same period last year.

The Americas as a region already lost its measles-free verification in November 2025, after Canada's outbreak ran past 12 months. PAHO said Tuesday that Canada has since interrupted the transmission chain tied to that outbreak and is working to regain elimination status, which is not automatic.

## What U.S. health officials said

The CDC issued a [joint statement](https://www.cdc.gov/media/releases/2026/loss-of-usmeasles-elimination-status.html) on November 17 with public health partners, saying the agency is "committed to stopping measles outbreaks, protecting Americans, and regaining measles elimination status."

CDC Director Dr. Erica Schwartz said: "The measles elimination designation is not what protects Americans. Vaccination does. We know where transmission is occurring and we've invested $100 million in state and local health departments to reach those communities. Losing a designation doesn't mean we abandon an evidence-based approach. It means we remain focused on stopping outbreaks."

The statement says that since August, CDC has provided about $100 million in additional funding to strengthen prevention and response to measles and other vaccine-preventable diseases through most of 2027, including $50.2 million through a vaccine-preventable disease cooperative agreement and $49.8 million through the Epidemiology and Laboratory Capacity cooperative agreement. It also says "Secretary Kennedy and Dr. Schwartz continue to recommend MMR vaccination as the most effective way to prevent measles."

The statement attributes part of the vaccination gap to COVID-era disruptions, saying MMR coverage among U.S. kindergartners fell from 95.2% in the 2019–2020 school year to 92.7% in 2023–2024. The CDC's measles data page, updated November 13, gives a later figure: coverage fell to 92.4% in the 2025–2026 school year, leaving about 280,000 kindergartners at risk.

White House spokesperson Allison Schuster, in an email to [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/public-health/us-measles-elimination-status-lost-outbreaks-continuous-spread-year-paho-cdc/), said: "The President and the Secretary have repeatedly encouraged measles vaccination, coordinated with states, and facilitated the deployment of vaccine doses across the country."

Some public health figures quoted by KFF Health News, including former CDC officials, have blamed Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and the administration for mixed messaging on vaccines and cuts to immunization programs. HHS and the White House have defended the administration's response.

## How the US gets it back

PAHO says a country regains elimination status only by showing it has interrupted endemic transmission for at least 12 consecutive months. It also has to show it has strengthened the systems that keep measles from taking hold again, including high and even vaccination coverage, strong epidemiological and laboratory surveillance, faster case detection, a rapid outbreak response, and a corrective action plan. The country submits that evidence to the commission, which recommends a classification to the PAHO Director.

PAHO's target is at least 95% coverage with two doses of measles vaccine "in every community, not simply at the national level."

It has been done before. PAHO points to Brazil, which lost its status in 2019, interrupted endemic transmission in 2022, and regained measles-free status in November 2024.

For families, PAHO's advice is to check that everyone has had two doses of a measles-containing vaccine, and to contact a doctor or health facility before showing up in person with a fever and rash.
