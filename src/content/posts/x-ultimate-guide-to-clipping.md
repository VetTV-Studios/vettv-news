X's official Creators account published an X Article titled "The ultimate guide to clipping" on Monday afternoon, November 23. The [post](https://x.com/XCreators/status/2124968363000582386) went up at 2:48 p.m. CT and links to the [article](https://x.com/i/article/2124966247574228992). The guide explains how X's native video clipping feature works, how clips are credited, how both the original creator and the clipper can earn through Original Content Rewards, and where clipping is available.

The feature itself was announced two weeks earlier. On November 9, at 2:37 p.m. CT, [@XCreators](https://x.com/XCreators/status/2119892170224074832) posted "Clipping is here" and said people could "grab your favorite moment from any video, add your take, and post it without leaving X." A [follow-up post](https://x.com/XCreators/status/2119892172757491898) one second later said clipping was "available on iOS and Android on any video longer than 2 minutes, and clips can run from 5 to 60 seconds," and linked to X's Help Center page, [About clipping on X](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/clipping).

## What the guide says clipping is

The guide defines a clip as "a short moment from someone's video, shared in your own post with your commentary." It says clipping "doesn't upload a new copy of the video." Instead, "Your clip plays the moment you picked straight from the original post, and every clip carries a Clip from label that links back to the full video."

X lists three results of that design:

- "Creators get credit, every time."
- "Viewers can keep watching. One tap on the label takes them to the original video."
- "Everyone can earn." Clippers earn from their clips, and the original creator earns from clips of their video, through Original Content Rewards.

## The rules, and where it works

According to the November 23 guide:

- Eligible videos are "at least 2 minutes long."
- Clip length is "5 to 60 seconds."
- "Live broadcasts can't be clipped, yet."
- Clipping is available in "the X app on iOS and Android, and on the web at x.com."

That last point is broader than the November 9 how-to post, which listed iOS and Android only. The new guide does not describe the web listing as a separate launch, and it does not give a date for when web clipping started. If you don't see Clip video, the guide says to update the X app to the latest version, or refresh x.com, and check that the video is at least 2 minutes long.

The guide also says the trimmer "lets you know when you've hit the maximum length for that video." It does not spell out a percentage cap in the article itself. X points readers to the Help Center for "the full details."

## How to make a clip

The official guide's steps are:

- Open a video that's at least 2 minutes long.
- Tap Repost, then Clip video.
- Drag the handles on the trimmer to choose where the clip starts and ends.
- Tap Next.
- Add your commentary, then post.

## Credit and playback

Every clip shows a "Clip from" label with the original post. The guide says viewers can tap it to open the original "right at the clipped moment, with the clip highlighted on the progress bar." It describes this as turning every clip into "a trailer for the full video."

## How earnings work

"Both the original creator and the clipper can earn from views on a clip through Original Content Rewards," the guide says.

- If you clip a video: "engagement on your clip counts toward your earnings, the same way your other posts do."
- If your video gets clipped: "you earn from clips of your video. These earnings are calculated the same way as earnings from embedded reposts of your content."
- "Each person needs to be enrolled in Original Content Rewards to earn from a clip."
- "Clip earnings are included in your regular Original Content Rewards payouts."

The guide also warns: "Downloading someone else's video and posting it as your own can be flagged as unoriginal content, which can affect your Original Content Rewards earnings." It says a native clip "does the same job, credits the creator, and lets you both earn."

The guide does not state a payout rate or a split between the two sides.

## Advice in the guide

The guide includes separate advice for clippers and for creators. Those points are X's recommendations, not ours.

For clippers, it says to pick a moment that stands on its own, start on the action, and keep the clip tight. "Just because you can clip 60 seconds doesn't mean you should," it says. "A sharp 20-second clip is easier to watch to the end, easier to rewatch, and easier to share." It also tells clippers to add their own take, move fast when a big video drops, find a lane, look for a moment everyone else missed, and write the post for people scrolling with the sound off.

For creators, it says to post long-form video natively on X, because clipping works on videos that are 2 minutes or longer. It also recommends building in standout moments, adding timestamps (which "turn into clickable links"), burning in captions so they show up in clips, making the full video worth the tap, engaging with people who clip your work, and clipping your own videos over the following days, "each one linking back to the full video."

## What still isn't available

Under "Good to know," X says:

- "Turning off clipping: you can't turn off clipping on your videos yet. We're working on it."
- "Deleting a video: if you delete your original video, its clips are removed too."
- "Livestreams: live broadcasts can't be clipped yet."

**Our take (opinion):** the guide is a full product pitch for clipping as distribution and a shared payout, and X is still shipping it without a creator opt-out. Until that control arrives, the only sure way the guide describes to remove clips is to delete the original video, which also takes the clips down.
