The Supreme Court on Monday, November 9, cleared the way for the Trump administration to restart border barrier and road construction in the Big Bend region of West Texas, an area that includes Big Bend National Park. In a five-page unsigned opinion, the justices put on hold a preliminary injunction that had stopped the work since early October, per the [Supreme Court's opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/26pdf/26a560_onkq.pdf) in *Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church* (No. 26A560).

Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the only justice noted as voting to deny the government's request, according to the [court's docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26A560.html). She did not write a separate dissent.

## What the court decided

The case turns on a 1996 immigration law. Section 102 of the Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act lets the Homeland Security secretary waive "all legal requirements" needed to build border barriers and roads quickly, and it only allows lawsuits over those waivers that claim a violation of the Constitution, the [opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/26pdf/26a560_onkq.pdf) explains. The same law sends appeals straight to the Supreme Court, skipping the Fifth Circuit.

The challengers argued the waivers violated the "major questions doctrine," which they framed as a constitutional claim. U.S. District Judge Kathleen Cardone in El Paso agreed it qualified and issued the injunction on October 2, per the [opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/26pdf/26a560_onkq.pdf) and [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/supreme-court/supreme-court-trump-border-wall-big-bend-texas-rcna604916). The Supreme Court disagreed, writing that the government is "exceedingly likely to prevail" on its argument that the district court lacked jurisdiction. "There is no such thing as a 'constitutional' major-questions claim," the justices wrote.

The court also pointed to a Customs and Border Protection declaration saying the injunction was costing the government nearly half a million dollars a day that it could not get back, and said Congress's waiver law reflects "a clear preference for expeditious border construction" over the environmental and recreational harms the challengers described, per the [opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/26pdf/26a560_onkq.pdf).

This is not a final ruling on whether the waivers are legal. The stay lasts until the government's petition for Supreme Court review is filed and decided, and it ends automatically if the court declines to take the case, per the [docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26A560.html).

## Who sued, and why

The plaintiffs are the Center for Biological Diversity, Friends of the Ruidosa Church, a nonprofit preserving a historic church in Ruidosa, Texas, and Danny Miller, a river guide who owns property in the area, per [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/supreme-court/supreme-court-trump-border-wall-big-bend-texas-rcna604916). They are challenging waivers issued by then-Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem in February and by her successor, Markwayne Mullin, in May and July, NBC reported.

The government says the project covers 341 miles of construction, [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/supreme-court/supreme-court-trump-border-wall-big-bend-texas-rcna604916) reported. The Center for Biological Diversity says the ruling lets work resume in Big Bend National Park and along more than 500 miles of the Rio Grande, per its [statement](https://biologicaldiversity.org/w/news/press-releases/supreme-court-clears-way-for-border-wall-construction-across-big-bend-2026-11-09/). The group says CBP's Big Bend Sector covers 517 miles of the border and accounted for 1.3% of border apprehensions in 2025. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/09/nx-s1-6019424/border-wall-big-bend-texas-trump) reported that the sector regularly records the fewest encounters along the Texas border, according to CBP data.

"This horrific decision clears the way for contractors to bulldoze pristine desert and dynamite canyon walls before the courts have even decided whether this destruction is legal," Laiken Jordahl, national public lands advocate at the Center for Biological Diversity, said in the group's [statement](https://biologicaldiversity.org/w/news/press-releases/supreme-court-clears-way-for-border-wall-construction-across-big-bend-2026-11-09/).

## What the administration says

In its emergency application, the government called the injunction "baseless" and "unprecedented," and Solicitor General D. John Sauer argued that any unbarriered stretch of Big Bend would become a "funnel point" for illegal crossings blocked elsewhere, per [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/09/nx-s1-6019424/border-wall-big-bend-texas-trump).

DHS General Counsel James Percival celebrated on X. "When we filed in the Supreme Court, I called the district court's order halting wall construction 'as unprecedented as it is flawed.' We just won 8-1," he wrote, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/supreme-court/supreme-court-trump-border-wall-big-bend-texas-rcna604916).

"We are moving forward with critical border security construction in West Texas," CBP Commissioner Rodney S. Scott told [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2026/11/09/texans-supreme-court-decision-big-bend-border-wall/92698754007/). He said the agency had "reevaluated the terrain, listened to local stakeholders, and made meaningful adjustments" and would share updated plans soon.

## Opposition from both parties

The project has drawn opposition across party lines, [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/09/nx-s1-6019424/border-wall-big-bend-texas-trump) reported. Outgoing Republican Sen. John Cornyn argued in a [San Antonio Express-News column](https://www.expressnews.com/opinion/commentary/article/big-bend-border-wall-cornyn-22520858.php) published Monday that "a border wall is not the best solution for portions of this environment" and urged DHS to use sensors, cameras and surveillance towers instead. Rep.-elect Katy Padilla Stout, a Democrat who will represent Texas' 23rd District, said in a statement, "No Wall At Big Bend. No Exceptions," per [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2026/11/09/texans-supreme-court-decision-big-bend-border-wall/92698754007/).

## What happens next

Construction can now resume while the lawsuit continues. The Center for Biological Diversity says crews began bulldozing near Santa Elena Canyon in Big Bend National Park on August 6, and that CBP paused work on August 17 before that pause lapsed September 30, per its [statement](https://biologicaldiversity.org/w/news/press-releases/supreme-court-clears-way-for-border-wall-construction-across-big-bend-2026-11-09/). A separate lawsuit filed by Texas landowners in September, claiming their constitutional rights are being violated, is also pending, [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/09/nx-s1-6019424/border-wall-big-bend-texas-trump) reported.

**Not yet confirmed as of 9 a.m. CT Tuesday:** when crews will return to work in the park, and what CBP's "updated plans" will include. CBP has not published them.
