Chile's competition prosecutor, the **Fiscalía Nacional Económica (FNE)**, said on **November 26** that it has filed a complaint, called a *requerimiento*, against **Apple Inc.** with the **Tribunal de Defensa de la Libre Competencia (TDLC)**, Chile's competition court. The [FNE's announcement](https://www.fne.gob.cl/fne-acusa-a-apple-por-abuso-de-posicion-dominante-y-pide-al-tdlc-multa-de-us-80-millones/) is dated November 26 with no time given, and it links the [filing itself](https://www.fne.gob.cl/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Requerimiento_FNE_en_contra_de_Apple_Inc_vf.pdf). [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/27/apple-faces-potential-80-million-fine-and-app-store-overhaul-in-chile/) reported it on November 27 at 2:31 p.m. CT.

## What the FNE alleges

The filing, signed by National Economic Prosecutor **Jorge Grunberg Pilowsky**, says Apple abused a dominant position "al menos desde el año 2019 a la actualidad" (at least from 2019 to the present) in two markets on iOS in Chile:

- **App distribution.** The FNE says Apple has blocked downloading apps through the browser (sideloading) and blocked app stores other than its own App Store.
- **Paid digital goods inside apps.** The FNE says Apple has required developers to sell digital goods only through its In-App Purchase billing system, and has banned telling users inside the app about other payment methods or outside promotions, which the filing calls the "anti-steering" rule.

According to the filing, these practices made the App Store the only way to get apps on iOS, degraded the experience for users, and let Apple secure profits it would not otherwise earn.

The FNE's announcement quotes Grunberg: "No es tolerable que las empresas abusen del poder que tienen en un mercado para cerrarlo" ("It is not tolerable for companies to abuse the power they have in a market to close it off"; our translation).

## What the FNE is asking for

The FNE asks the TDLC to order Apple to:

1. Allow sideloading of apps and third-party app stores on iOS devices, without discouraging messages or unjustified technical restrictions.
2. Allow alternative billing systems inside apps.
3. Drop the anti-steering rule, so developers can add in-app buttons or links to outside payment systems.
4. Let developers tell users, inside and outside their apps, about other billing systems, prices and promotions.

It also asks for a fine equal to 30% of Apple's sales in the affected lines during the period investigated. The FNE puts that at **92,378 UTA** (Chile's annual tax unit), which it says is **more than US$80 million**.

## What Apple has said

Apple had not responded publicly as of Friday afternoon. 9to5Mac said it had asked Apple for comment. These are the FNE's allegations; the TDLC has not ruled on them, and Apple will get to answer the complaint in court.

## What happens next

The case now goes to the TDLC, which will decide whether Apple broke Chile's competition law and whether to impose the remedies and fine the FNE wants. The FNE has not given a timeline. The case adds to app store fights elsewhere, including the [Epic v. Apple dispute in the US](/posts/epic-apple-scotus-32-states-microsoft-spotify-amicus/).
