Rumble has opened a Partner Program that it says lets creators "keep up to 90% of ad revenue." Creator posts celebrating acceptances spread across X on November 5.

CEO Chris Pavlovski [announced](https://x.com/chrispavlovski/status/2115897789158773217) the change on October 29: "On November 1st, we're flipping the script — we're going 90/10 on eligible video ads." He said creators "will get up to 90% revenue share on eligible video pre-rolls, mid-rolls, and host-read sponsorship ads." In a [follow-up post](https://x.com/chrispavlovski/status/2115966882159435899), he said the program is for both VOD and livestream creators.

## What Rumble confirms

- **Invitation only:** Rumble's [program page](https://go.rumble.com/studio/partner-program/) says it's "by invitation only." Creators request access, a partnerships team reviews each request, and approved creators get their invitation and terms by email.
- **Up to 90%:** the page says "keep up to 90% of ad revenue," and Pavlovski describes it as "90/10 on eligible video ads."
- **Requirements:** "Stream or upload at least 10 times using Rumble Studio over the last 30 day window." There are "no subscriber or watch-hour minimums."
- **Staying Active:** Rumble's [help article](https://rumble.support/help/rumble-partner-program), last updated November 3, says a video counts if it's published from Rumble Studio, public and approved for monetization. The count uses a rolling 30-day window. If it drops below 10, benefits pause, but you stay in the program.
- **Benefits:** Active Partners get a higher ad share on all their videos, not only those made in Studio, plus a higher share of Studio host-read revenue and a daily "Premium Share" based on how much Rumble Premium subscribers watch their content.
- **Optional and free:** joining costs nothing, and creators outside the program keep earning as they do today.

## What isn't public yet

Rumble says "exact terms are shared one-on-one with approved creators." It hasn't publicly defined which ads count as "eligible," or what decides whether a creator gets the full 90% or less. **Unconfirmed:** Posthype (secondary) reports that Rumble's public terms of use, as it read them on November 2, still listed a 40% agency fee and other cost deductions. We haven't independently checked how those terms interact with the new program. WIN.gg (secondary) reported on Pavlovski's message to creators about the change.

*Opinion:* a low bar of 10 videos a month with no audience minimums is a real draw for smaller streamers. But until the "eligible ads" definition and the private terms are public, "up to 90%" is a ceiling, not a guarantee.
