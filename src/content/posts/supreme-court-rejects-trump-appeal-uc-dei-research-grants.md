The Supreme Court on Monday, November 16, declined to hear the Trump administration's appeal in *Trump v. Thakur*, a fight over federal research grants that were ended for University of California researchers under President Donald Trump's executive orders on diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI). The case, No. 26-210, appears in the "Certiorari Denied" section of the Court's [November 16 order list](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/111626zor_cb7d.pdf). The order gives no reasons, and no justice is noted as dissenting.

Turning the case away leaves in place a May 26, 2026 ruling by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, which upheld a preliminary injunction for one class of UC researchers. A denial of review is not a Supreme Court ruling on the merits. The Ninth Circuit decision stands, and the case goes on in the lower courts.

## What the lower courts decided

The researchers sued in June 2025 in federal court in the Northern District of California. On June 23, 2025, U.S. District Judge Rita F. Lin provisionally certified two classes of UC researchers and issued a preliminary injunction ordering the grant terminations set aside and the grants reinstated, according to the [government's petition](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-210/419403/20260818155050746_Trump_v_Thakur_petn_final.pdf), which reprints both lower court opinions.

The part of the case that reached the Supreme Court covers what the courts called the "Equity Termination Class": UC researchers whose National Science Foundation (NSF) or National Endowment for the Humanities (NEH) grants were ended under Executive Orders 14151 or 14173, the two DEI orders, on or after January 20, 2025. The Environmental Protection Agency was also a defendant, but the petition says the researchers reached a settlement in principle with EPA in July 2026.

A Ninth Circuit panel of Judges Richard A. Paez, Morgan B. Christen and Roopali H. Desai issued a per curiam opinion siding with the researchers on that class. "[W]here the government establishes a subsidy program, it may not discriminate between speakers within that program to suppress viewpoints with which it disagrees," the panel wrote. The researchers had argued that the government "has not merely exercised its power to choose the programs it funds but has instead terminated individual grants in existing programs based on the perceived viewpoints of individual speakers." The panel's answer: "We agree with Plaintiffs," per the opinion reprinted in the [petition](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-210/419403/20260818155050746_Trump_v_Thakur_petn_final.pdf).

The same panel ruled against the researchers on a second group, the "Form Termination Class," whose grants were ended by form letters. It said those claims likely belong in the Court of Federal Claims, not federal district court. That part of the ruling was not before the Supreme Court.

## What each side argued

In its [petition](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-210/419403/20260818155050746_Trump_v_Thakur_petn_final.pdf), filed August 18 by Solicitor General D. John Sauer, the administration framed the question as whether the First Amendment bars the government "from considering the viewpoints furthered by academic research projects when deciding whether to grant or terminate funding for those projects through competitive grant programs." It argued that the Ninth Circuit's ruling conflicts with Supreme Court precedents including *Rust v. Sullivan* and *National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley*, and that leaving it in place would set "a dangerous precedent that will hamstring every future administration when seeking to subsidize private projects that promote viewpoints aligned with government policy." The petition says the NSF and NEH grants in this case had about $59 million left on them when they were terminated, with some running as late as November 2029.

The researchers, represented by counsel of record Erwin Chemerinsky of UC Berkeley School of Law, argued in their [brief in opposition](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-210/429878/20261019113659342_26-210%20Brief%20in%20Opposition.pdf) that the case is about grants that had already been awarded. "It is one thing for Congress to set priorities in funding and quite another for the President to terminate already approved funding because of the viewpoints perceived to be expressed in the research," they wrote. They told the Court there is no split among the lower courts and urged the justices to let the issue develop in other appeals first. Their brief also says the government ended more than $324 million in grants to the UC system between January 20 and early June 2025. That figure is the researchers' claim, citing their complaint, and is not a finding by the Supreme Court.

The government filed a [reply](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-210/430848/20261027184347402_26-210_Trump_v_Thakur_cert_reply_final.pdf) on October 27, and the case was distributed for the justices' November 13 conference, according to the [docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-210.html).

## What is still open

- **The merits:** The injunction is preliminary. The Ninth Circuit held only that the researchers are likely to succeed on their First Amendment claim, and the case continues in district court.
- **Other agencies:** The petition says a second injunction covering grants ended by the Departments of Transportation, Defense and Health and Human Services relies on the same reasoning, and that the government's appeal of it was being held in abeyance pending this appeal. What happens to that appeal now has not been announced.
- **Parallel cases:** The petition and the opposition brief both point to a similar NEH case, *American Council of Learned Societies v. NEH*, in which a federal court in New York entered a classwide permanent injunction in May 2026. Appeals in that case are pending in the Second Circuit.

As of Monday morning, VetTV News had not found a public statement on the order from the White House, the Justice Department, NSF, NEH or the University of California.
