The official Muse account, [@Muse](https://x.com/Muse/status/2120931418654089392), posted on November 12 at 11:27 a.m. CT (5:27 p.m. UTC): "Let Muse handle the wait times while you get back some free time. Introducing outbound calling, available now for paid US subscribers."

Meta chief AI officer [Alexandr Wang](https://x.com/alexandr_wang/status/2120930586827391350) posted a few minutes earlier, at 11:23 a.m. CT: "muse can make phone calls for you," and "let muse take care of all those calls you don't want to make. insurance? cable? DMV? dinner res? not your problem anymore." He closed with, "out now to paid subs in the US."

Those official posts limit the feature to paid subscribers in the United States. They do not mention Canada or other countries, and they do not say outbound calling is available on a free plan.

[TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/muse-can-now-place-calls-to-u-s-businesses-if-you-pay/) the same day, at 2:22 p.m. PST (4:22 p.m. CT), that Muse "is now able to place calls to U.S. businesses on behalf of its users" and that "only paid subscribers have access to the feature." The official Muse and Wang posts describe outbound calling for paid U.S. subscribers and give examples of calls; they do not themselves use the phrase "U.S. businesses." TechCrunch also writes that Muse plans "start at $20 per month for 500 million Muse tokens per week" and that a higher plan is "$100 per month for 3 billion tokens." Those prices and token amounts were not confirmed on a Meta help or pricing page for this report, so they are TechCrunch's figures, not a Meta page we could quote.

Wang's examples are insurance, cable, the DMV, and a dinner reservation. The official Muse post highlights waiting on hold. Neither official post describes how Muse identifies itself on a call, or whether the feature will later expand beyond paid U.S. subscribers.
