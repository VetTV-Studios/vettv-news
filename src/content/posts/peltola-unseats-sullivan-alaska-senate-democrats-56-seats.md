Democrat Mary Peltola has won Alaska's U.S. Senate race, unseating two-term Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan. [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/mary-peltola-democrat-wins-alaska-senate-dan-sullivan-gop-rcna603307) and the [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/alaska-senate-peltola-sullivan-congress-f1fd4d243bf764ec46ff94db4c792953) called the race for Peltola Tuesday night, November 17. It was the last Senate contest of the 2026 midterms to be resolved.

Peltola claimed victory on X at 9:23 PM CT. "As a lifelong Alaskan who has dedicated my career to serving our state, there is no greater honor than having Alaskans' support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs, and put Alaska first," she [wrote on X](https://x.com/MaryPeltola/status/2122893455164871164). "Thank you, Alaska. Let's get to work." She followed with a video post: ["We did it, Alaska!"](https://x.com/MaryPeltola/status/2122900390563959210)

## The official numbers

The [Alaska Division of Elections' summary report](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/enr/26GENR/results/ElectionSummaryReportRPT.pdf), generated November 17 with all 401 of 401 precincts reporting, shows:

- **Mary Peltola (DEM):** 144,302 votes, 50.12%
- **Dan S. Sullivan (REP):** 131,844 votes, 45.80%
- **Gerald L. Heikes (REP):** 6,105 votes, 2.12%
- **Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. (no party listed):** 4,760 votes, 1.65%
- **Write-in:** 885 votes, 0.31%

That is a 12,458-vote lead out of 287,896 votes cast in the race. Statewide, 290,003 ballots were cast, a turnout of 47.90% of 605,433 registered voters.

These results are still **unofficial**. The Division of Elections [says](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/election-information/) all results stay unofficial until the State Review Board certifies the election.

## Why there's no ranked-choice runoff

Alaska uses a top-four primary and ranked-choice general election. The Division of Elections says that under state law, tabulation continues round by round "unless a candidate achieves a majority in the first round." Peltola cleared 50% of first-choice votes, so the race ends in round one.

That also matters for the fight over the second Sullivan on the ballot. Republicans and the Trump administration argued that Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., a political newcomer, could pull votes away from the incumbent by confusion over the name. By VetTV News' count of the official totals, Peltola's 144,302 votes are more than Dan S. Sullivan, Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., Heikes and all write-ins combined (143,594). [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/mary-peltola-democrat-wins-alaska-senate-dan-sullivan-gop-rcna603307) reached the same conclusion: because Peltola won a first-round majority, the confusion over the two Sullivans did not affect the result.

## What it means for the Senate

According to the [AP](https://apnews.com/article/alaska-senate-peltola-sullivan-congress-f1fd4d243bf764ec46ff94db4c792953) and [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/mary-peltola-democrat-wins-alaska-senate-dan-sullivan-gop-rcna603307), Peltola's win gives Democrats a 56-44 Senate majority when the new Congress is seated, after the party flipped nine Republican-held seats in the midterms. NBC notes the 56 includes two independents who caucus with Democrats, Sens. Angus King of Maine and Bernie Sanders of Vermont. NBC calls it the largest Senate majority either party has had in 16 years.

Peltola, 53, is a former U.S. House member. She won Alaska's at-large House seat in a 2022 special election after the death of Republican Rep. Don Young and then lost it to Republican Nick Begich in 2024. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/17/mary-peltola-alaska-senate-race) reports she is the first Democrat Alaska has elected to the Senate since 2008. The Guardian also reports that Peltola, who is Yup'ik, and Minnesota Senator-elect Peggy Flanagan will be the first two Native American women to serve in the Senate.

Senate Democrats' campaign arm, the DSCC, [congratulated](https://x.com/dscc/status/2122900848162304022) "Senator-Elect Mary Peltola" on X Tuesday night.

## Sullivan has not conceded

Sullivan has not conceded. In a statement Monday, November 16, before the race was called, campaign spokesperson Nate Adams said, as quoted by [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/US/2026/11/18/Mary-Peltola-claims-Alaska-Senate-victory/4501794976433/) and [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/mary-peltola-democrat-wins-alaska-senate-dan-sullivan-gop-rcna603307): "As Senator Sullivan and his campaign have said since Election Day, Alaskans deserve a complete and accurate count, and we will let that process run its course until every legal vote is counted and certified." Adams added that the campaign would not comment on "individual ballot updates or outside projections" and would "have more to say by the end of this week." As of this writing, VetTV News found no new post about the result on Sullivan's Senate office X account.

## The legal fights still open

The race was shadowed for months by the candidacy of Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. The New York Times reported that Peltola campaign staffers were involved in recruiting him, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/mary-peltola-democrat-wins-alaska-senate-dan-sullivan-gop-rcna603307) and [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/17/mary-peltola-alaska-senate-race). Peltola has denied involvement, and Sullivan Jr. has said running was his own decision. Alaska's Supreme Court allowed his name to stay on the ballot.

In October, the Justice Department sued to block Alaska from certifying the race if the Republican incumbent lost. U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed that suit on November 1, and the department filed notice that it would appeal, [the AP reported](https://apnews.com/article/alaska-senate-dan-sullivan-peltola-investigation-b35acffc8b55ef58679d9b8395760f98). A department spokesperson said then: "Alaskan voters deserve a fair election, not to be misled in an attempt to rig outcomes." [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2026/11/17/peltola-win-doesnt-end-doj-scrutiny-alaska-senate-race-officials-say/) reported Tuesday that Justice Department officials say a criminal grand jury investigation into the alleged recruitment is still underway and that the civil appeal continues. VetTV News found no announced charges as of Wednesday morning.

With Peltola above 50% on first-choice votes, the remaining open questions are certification by the State Review Board and whether any recount request or legal challenge follows.
