Hours after the National Capital Planning Commission approved final site and building plans for President Donald Trump's proposed triumphal arch, U.S. District Judge Tanya S. Chutkan granted a 14-day temporary restraining order sought by the District of Columbia and the Commonwealth of Virginia. The [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/11/05/trump-dc-arch-approval-construction-lawsuit/92637101007/) report, updated November 6, says the order bars the administration from initiating construction while the court considers the case. Both the NCPC vote and the TRO came on Thursday, November 5, 2026.

## The NCPC vote

The commission voted 8–4 to approve the plans after a long public hearing. [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/stocks-blue-wave-ai-construction-trump-arch-morning-rundown-rcna607801) reported more than nine hours of public comments and said all four no votes came from commissioners not appointed by Trump. [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/6202351-ncpc-approves-trump-arch/) similarly described a nearly nine-hour meeting and an 8–4 approval. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/trump-washington-arch-planning-commission-246d15697c00b065bc8ab8c614d50ca7) coverage of the same vote and the ensuing 14-day delay is consistent on the timeline.

Speaker turnout figures vary by outlet. USA Today described about 100 opponents addressing the panel; [The Washington Sun](https://www.washingtonsun.com/metro/trump-triumphal-arch-national-capital-planning-commission-vote) said 131 people signed up to speak. **Unconfirmed:** an exact headcount of speakers who were heard.

NCPC vice chair Stuart Levenbach, a Trump appointee, told the meeting the proposed arch would turn a "tired traffic circle" into a "monumental gateway," according to [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/6202351-ncpc-approves-trump-arch/).

Rep. Don Beyer (D-Va.), opposing the project at the hearing, said it would "snarl traffic for years, impact the nearby airport, and present even more serious threats to public safety that this commission has refused to acknowledge and grapple with," USA Today reported.

## What the TRO blocks

Chutkan's order, as quoted by the Washington Sun and summarized by USA Today, prohibits for 14 days initiating, making, or otherwise effectuating construction, excavation, demolition, land-disturbance, utility-installation, roadway-closure, or other construction-related activity at or near Memorial Circle for the purpose of building the arch.

USA Today reports that Chutkan directed federal officials to notify employees, contractors, subcontractors, and other entities that construction-related activity is prohibited, and that defendants must update the court by November 9 on those notification efforts and certify compliance. The TRO is temporary; it does not decide the merits of the lawsuits.

## The lawsuits

The District of Columbia and Virginia sued the National Park Service and the Department of the Interior on October 31, seeking to stop construction before it began, according to USA Today. Their complaint called the project "legally indefensible" and argued Congress never authorized the monument. The Trump administration contends Congress approved the arch more than a century ago when it authorized a bridge across the Potomac River, USA Today reported.

A separate earlier suit by Vietnam War veterans and an architectural historian, *Lemmon v. Trump*, No. 26-cv-544 (D.D.C.), was consolidated with the D.C./Virginia case, No. 26-cv-3838, before Judge Chutkan, according to the [Washington Post court memorandum PDF](https://www.washingtonpost.com/documents/d0fa2bb9-3367-4379-a56e-67cf1a5bb048.pdf) captioning the consolidated cases.

## The proposed arch

The monument would sit at Memorial Circle / Columbus Island on the bridge linking the Lincoln Memorial to Arlington National Cemetery, per USA Today and NPR summaries. Modeled after Paris's Arc de Triomphe, it would stand about 250 feet tall — more than 80 feet taller than the French arch and nearly the height of the 288-foot U.S. Capitol, according to USA Today.

The Department of Justice had previously said an initial six to eight months of excavation and foundation work could begin as early as that week, while the National Park Service could issue final permits, USA Today reported. NBC said the administration had expected construction to begin immediately after approval. **Unconfirmed:** any claim that excavation was literally scheduled for hours after the vote; outlets describe administration timing statements, not a confirmed start clock.

Speaking November 2 at a Reflecting Pool event, Trump said the arch could house drones and snipers: "It's right smack in the middle of everything, so very important for our military. But from a beautification standpoint, it'll be the most beautiful arch in the world," USA Today quoted.

USA Today notes the arch is one of several Trump D.C. projects facing local and preservation opposition, alongside Reflecting Pool work, a White House ballroom, and Kennedy Center changes.

## What happens next

The restraining order lasts 14 days while the court considers the case. Nothing in the order settles whether the arch is permanently blocked or eventually built. A final ruling on the consolidated challenges has not been issued.
