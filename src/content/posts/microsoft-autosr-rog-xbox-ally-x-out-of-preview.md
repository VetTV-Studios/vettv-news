Microsoft said on **November 10, 2026**, that Automatic Super Resolution (**Auto SR**) is officially out of preview on the **ROG Xbox Ally X** and the **ROG Xbox Ally X20** (Microsoft's post styles the names as ROG XBOX Ally X and ROG XBOX Ally X20). The company also says Auto SR is now available on **select Windows Copilot+ PCs powered by AMD Ryzen**.

## What is available now, and what is still rolling out

Microsoft draws a line between features available today and those arriving later through Windows updates:

- **Available today:** handheld play undocked on battery, support for games running at **1080p**, and the expanded Copilot+ PC support on select AMD Ryzen systems.
- **Rolling out with Windows updates:** a new **Performance Mode** that Microsoft says can run at **up to 120 FPS**, and the ability to use Auto SR together with **Auto HDR**.

Until now, Microsoft says, Auto SR topped out around **60 to 75 FPS**, and the Xbox Insider preview was limited to docked play. The company also says players previously had to lower a game's resolution before Auto SR would turn on; 1080p titles can now use it, and Microsoft says docking to a higher-resolution display can upscale up to **1440p**. A note in the post adds that 1080p on the internal handheld screen "may not be as optimal as other configurations."

The **up to 120 FPS** figure, and Microsoft's side-by-side **Rocket League** examples on a ROG Xbox Ally X at 720p High (Quality Mode averaging **70 FPS**, Performance Mode averaging **115 FPS**), are **Microsoft's own claims and screenshots**. They are not independent tests.

Microsoft says players should update Game Bar and Windows 11, install or update the Auto Super Resolution package from the Microsoft Store, then set Quality or Performance in Game Bar under Widgets > Display > Graphics.
