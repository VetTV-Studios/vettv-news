The Federal Aviation Administration, in a [Questions and Answers page dated Sunday, November 15](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/nationwide-ground-stop-questions-and-answers), said a technical problem at about 3:30 p.m. ET on Friday, November 13 (2:30 p.m. CT) "resulted in issuing a nationwide ground stop." "The system was made stable and air traffic resumed as of 8:15 p.m. that same day" (7:15 p.m. CT), the FAA said.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy briefed reporters on Friday night, according to a [White House release](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/11/icymi-after-nationwide-ground-stop-trump-administration-prioritizes-safety-calls-for-full-modernization-funding/). He put the start of the instability at 3:36 p.m.: "At 3:36 p.m., we experienced instability for our radar and our communications in our centers and our TRACONs across the country… At that point, we issued a ground stop."

## What the FAA says happened

The Sunday Q&A says "the FAA's legacy equipment became unstable when data began flooding the FAA FTI network, at a rate of 1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds." That "disrupted the system and created instability with aircraft communications and radar feeds."

"The initial investigation indicates that two potential locations" were the source of that data flood: "a radar site in Amarillo, Texas and a radio communications site in El Dorado, Arkansas." The FAA says L3Harris, "the FAA's current network service provider, had been doing work at the El Dorado site, including deployment of a new router." After the data inconsistencies appeared, "L3 Harris blocked those two locations and the network stabilized." The agency also "disabled" the "broadcasting function… on all the new routers as a precaution," and later said it disabled the same functionality "on approximately 60 similar routers in the system."

The FAA says instability began "at 17 of our 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers, and it spread into several Terminal Radar Approach Controls." It describes this as "not a full outage," saying some radio frequencies and radar feeds "remained operational" or "reliable," and that "every aircraft landed safely, and all passengers arrived safely." "Approximately 3,500 aircraft safely landed," the Q&A says.

On cancellations, the FAA says that on Friday, "operators canceled approximately 12,462 flights, including 9,082 cancellations by major airlines," and adds: "While not all cancellations were directly caused by the nationwide ground stop, all flights landed safely."

## What is still under investigation

"The FAA and L3Harris continue to investigate the root cause," the Sunday Q&A says. Asked whether the event was a cyberattack, the FAA answered: "Based on all available evidence, we have no indication that this was a cyberattack or an outside nefarious actor."

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, quoted in the White House release, said Friday night: "We have seen zero evidence that this was a cyberattack or an SI attack." He also said, "It appears that this was mechanically driven; however, we're still looking."

The FAA says the disruption was "due to the legacy TDM system causing instabilities," not its broader modernization work or the SMART system, and that "none of the upgraded towers that received modifications from the down payment in the Working Families Tax Cuts went down." It also says it will "reevaluate when we do replacement/maintenance work," after being asked why the router replacement happened during the day.

## The funding argument

Duffy used the Friday briefing to press Congress for more air-traffic-control funding. "The President, for the last year, has been asking Congress to fully fund a full build of a brand-new air traffic control system," he said in the White House release. "I want $50 billion." He said he had already received "$12.5 billion" through "One Big Beautiful Bill," "but the Congress at the time knew that that was not going to be enough." Mullin said, "This is what happens when Congress kicks the can down the road." Those are the administration's arguments; the Sunday FAA Q&A does not itself request a dollar figure.
