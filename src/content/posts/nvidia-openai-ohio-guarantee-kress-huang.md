The Wall Street Journal [reported on November 12](https://wccftech.com/jensen-huang-financed-ai-startups-absorb-excess-silicon/) that Colette Kress, then NVIDIA's chief financial officer, reworked a planned financial backstop for an OpenAI data-center project and cut the company's initial commitment in half. NVIDIA's own filings, signed the same summer, show what the company ultimately disclosed: residual-value guarantees capped at $105 billion for about 4.25 gigawatts of IT load at a campus in Pike County, Ohio.

Kress is now NVIDIA's interim chief executive, after Jensen Huang resigned in November. VetTV News covered that announcement [here](https://news.vettvstudios.com/posts/nvidia-jensen-huang-resigns-colette-kress-interim-ceo/).

## What NVIDIA filed

On August 17, 2026, NVIDIA [announced](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Guarantees-SB-Energys-PORTS-Pike-Technology-Campus-in-Ohio-to-Exclusively-Host-NVIDIA-AI-Compute/default.aspx) a partnership with SB Energy at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus. A [Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000069/nvda-20260817.htm) filed the same day says NVIDIA entered into multiple residual-value guaranties with SB Energy "relating to leases for approximately 4.25 gigawatts of IT load in the aggregate at the Portsmouth Site." An affiliate of OpenAI Group PBC "will be the tenant."

The 8-K says NVIDIA's "aggregate payment obligation is cumulatively capped at $105 billion for its initial commitment under the Agreements." That cap is not a $105 billion cash outlay. Under the filing, NVIDIA would pay a shortfall between a guaranteed minimum lease value and amounts recovered through a replacement lease or sale if OpenAI became insolvent and defaulted, or failed to make lease payments. OpenAI "has agreed to reimburse and indemnify NVIDIA for any and all amounts actually paid by NVIDIA to the Lessor under the Agreements."

The guarantees generally become effective when the applicable lease commences. The 8-K says payment obligations are subject to ready-for-service conditions "expected beginning in 2028." NVIDIA's [Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm) for the quarter ended July 26, 2026, filed August 26, describes nine phases of construction, "the first of which is expected in fiscal year 2029," with guarantee amounts increasing as phases are completed and exposure expected to decrease over each phase's 20-year lease term.

NVIDIA "also can provide credit support to secure approximately an additional 3.8 gigawatts at the Portsmouth Site, exercisable in its sole discretion," the 8-K says. The company's press release describes that option as "the remaining 3.75 IT-GW" and says OpenAI "will be the customer for 8-IT GW." The press release also says NVIDIA will invest $1.5 billion in SB Energy. In exchange for the guarantees, the 10-Q says, "the site will exclusively host NVIDIA AI infrastructure, subject to limited exceptions."

The same 10-Q lists maximum gross exposure of $108.5 billion on guarantees: $105.0 billion for the SB Energy guarantees and $3.5 billion for other "land, power, and shell guarantees for AI clouds." Separately, it says supply and capacity commitments rose from $119 billion in the prior quarter to $279 billion as of July 26, 2026, "primarily memory and manufacturing facilities."

## How the Journal says the OpenAI backstop was sized down

The Journal's November 12 article, by Anissa Gardizy and Robbie Whelan, says NVIDIA had been in talks with OpenAI on a financial backstop for an Ohio campus. On July 26, the Journal [reported](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/nvidia-in-talks-with-openai-to-guarantee-250-billion-financing-for-data-center-3dd6eae3) that NVIDIA was considering a $250 billion backstop for the project. The November article says NVIDIA shares fell 5% the next day, "wiping out $250 billion in market value." Those market figures are the Journal's.

Citing people familiar with the deal, the November article says that over the following weeks, "Kress reworked the deal and cut Nvidia's initial financial commitment to OpenAI in half," and that NVIDIA and OpenAI "signed the downsized deal on Aug. 17." An earlier Journal report, [published August 14](https://www.wsj.com/tech/nvidia-downsizes-plans-for-250-billion-guarantee-of-openai-data-center-b56c38d3), said the companies had reworked the structure so NVIDIA would initially guarantee only half of a planned multi-hundred-billion-dollar build-out, lowering the guarantee "from $250 billion to less than $120 billion," according to people familiar with the deal.

NVIDIA's August 17 filings are the signed figures. They do not themselves describe the internal negotiations.

## The August meeting, as the Journal reported it

The same November 12 article describes an August meeting in which Huang summoned senior finance executives, including Kress. According to a person familiar with the comments, the Journal reported, Huang interrupted Kress's presentation and called her "a deer in the headlights." It also reported that he called her a "reporting CFO," "not someone who could help him define a strategy or make decisions."

Those remarks are the Journal's account, attributed to a person familiar with the comments. NVIDIA has not confirmed them in the filings reviewed for this story.

The Journal, citing people familiar with the matter, wrote that Huang had launched a dealmaking effort to provide guarantees and credit support to companies buying its chips, including offers to "upstart cloud providers and data-center developers," and that he was "starting to question whether it had gone too far." In that meeting, the paper reported, Huang said major cloud providers were "running away from Nvidia, making plans to use alternative chips and lessen their reliance on the firm," and that "the vast supply of chip wafers and memory Nvidia had committed to buying were coming at him like an 'avalanche.'"

The Journal did not name a foundry in that passage, and it did not say NVIDIA contacted TSMC to wind down orders.

## Circular financing, and how NVIDIA answered it

Critics have described NVIDIA's investments and credit support for customers as "circular financing." In late August, the Journal reported, Kress addressed that criticism publicly: "We recognize the scale of this support, and we know some will call this circular financing. We see it differently. The equity returns on our invested capital will be excellent."

## What some coverage has added

A November 13 [Wccftech article](https://wccftech.com/jensen-huang-financed-ai-startups-absorb-excess-silicon/) cited the Journal report and argued that Huang "financed AI startups to absorb excess silicon" instead of lowering GPU prices, a framing it said "shatter[ed] the shortage perception." That is Wccftech's analysis. The Journal article does not say NVIDIA financed customers in order to keep consumer graphics-card or memory prices high, and the company's filings do not describe a strategy of creating artificial demand.

Wccftech also wrote that the signed guarantee cap was "up to $125 billion," and that NVIDIA "reportedly contacted TSMC to wind down its upcoming chip orders." The $125 billion figure does not match the $105 billion cap in the 8-K and 10-Q. The TSMC wind-down claim does not appear in the Journal article reviewed for this story.
