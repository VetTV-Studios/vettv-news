Instagram is putting Meta's Muse assistant into Direct Message threads. In a [November 24 post](https://x.com/instagram/status/2125295853816692960) at **12:29 p.m. CT**, the official Instagram account said: "Muse can now organize Instagram saves into collections or travel itineraries, find saved reels and turn recipes into grocery lists. Use it in DMs to learn more about a post or catch up on what you missed. Muse won't make changes or take action without your OK."

[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/meta-brings-its-muse-ai-assistant-directly-into-instagram-dms/), in a November 24 article by Aisha Malik timestamped 9:00 a.m. PT (11:00 a.m. CT), reported that Instagram users can chat with Muse in a DM thread "to get more context about a post, place, or product they come across, or catch up on conversations they may have missed in their other DMs." TechCrunch said users can share Instagram posts with Muse and ask it to take action, with examples such as adding a coffee shop to a Mexico City itinerary or finding a Black Friday deal on a pair of shoes.

TechCrunch, citing Meta, also said conversations with Muse on Instagram "will appear as a side chat in the main Muse app."

This is a separate rollout from Meta's [UK launch of Muse](/posts/meta-muse-uk-launch/), which we covered from Meta's November 23 newsroom post.

## Where it is rolling out

TechCrunch reported that Muse in Instagram DMs "will begin rolling out on Tuesday" for **iOS and Android** users in the **United States, Canada, Mexico, Japan, and Singapore**. We did not find a Meta Newsroom post for this feature; Instagram's X post is the official announcement we are treating as primary, with the country list coming from TechCrunch's report of what Meta said.

[The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1041374/you-can-now-dm-your-muse-ai-agent-on-instagram) also covered the news on November 24, pointing to a Meta video and citing TechCrunch for the country list.

## Collections, in the U.S. and Canada

Meta also said users can do more with bookmarked posts and Reels, TechCrunch reported: ask Muse to organize saved content into custom folders, known as **Collections**; turn a recipe video into a grocery list; turn saved travel content into an itinerary; or send outfit ideas based on fashion Reels.

Starting Tuesday, TechCrunch said, when users in the **United States and Canada** save a post on Instagram, they will see a prompt for Muse to organize it for them. That Collections prompt is narrower than the five-country DM rollout.

TechCrunch said Meta told it that "users remain in control of their Muse experience on Instagram. Nothing is shared, published, or sent without their approval, and they can ask Muse to forget information or undo actions at any time." That matches Instagram's public line that Muse will not make changes or take action without the user's OK.
