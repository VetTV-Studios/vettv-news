The Senate on Thursday, November 19, passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, a broad rewrite of how the federal government reviews and approves power lines, pipelines, mines, wind and solar farms and other infrastructure. The [official roll call](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1192/vote_119_2_00270.htm) shows it passed 62-33. The bill now goes to the House.

[Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/19/senate-passes-landmark-permitting-deal-01177557) called it the most significant energy legislation to clear the Senate since the Inflation Reduction Act in 2022.

Two Republicans and two Democrats wrote the bill: Sens. Shelley Moore Capito of West Virginia and Mike Lee of Utah, who chair the Environment and Public Works and the Energy and Natural Resources committees, and the top Democrats on those panels, Sens. Sheldon Whitehouse of Rhode Island and Martin Heinrich of New Mexico. They introduced it as S. 5653 on Sept. 30, [according to GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-119s5653is).

## How the Senate voted

According to the [Senate roll call](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1192/vote_119_2_00270.htm):

- **Yes (62):** 47 Republicans, 14 Democrats and independent Angus King of Maine.
- **No (33):** 30 Democrats, independent Bernie Sanders of Vermont, and Republicans Josh Hawley of Missouri and Mike Rounds of South Dakota.
- **Not voting (5):** Republicans Bill Cassidy of Louisiana, John Cornyn of Texas, Mitch McConnell of Kentucky and Tommy Tuberville of Alabama, and Democrat Chris Murphy of Connecticut.

The 14 Democrats who voted yes were Chris Coons, John Fetterman, Ruben Gallego, Martin Heinrich, John Hickenlooper, Amy Klobuchar, Gary Peters, Jack Reed, Jacky Rosen, Brian Schatz, Chuck Schumer, Elissa Slotkin, Mark Warner and Sheldon Whitehouse. [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/11/19/sweeping-permitting-overhaul-passes-senate-on-to-house), the [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/climate-environment/2026/11/19/senate-votes-weaken-environmental-laws-speed-up-energy-projects/) and [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/20/g-s1-154579/senate-bipartisan-energy-bill) reported matching totals, with Roll Call counting Sanders among 31 members of the Democratic caucus who voted no.

The Senate used a House-passed bill, H.R. 2347, as the vehicle and replaced its text with the permitting bill, a common procedure. That is why the roll call is listed under H.R. 2347, and why the bill must go back to the House. Earlier test votes were also lopsided: the Senate [invoked cloture on the substitute](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1192/vote_119_2_00264.htm) 70-27 on Nov. 16, [adopted it](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1192/vote_119_2_00267.htm) 69-27 on Nov. 18, and [invoked cloture on the bill](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1192/vote_119_2_00268.htm) 65-31 the same day.

## What the bill does

[Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/11/19/sweeping-permitting-overhaul-passes-senate-on-to-house) reports that the bill changes how reviews are done and permits are issued under the National Environmental Policy Act, the National Historic Preservation Act, the Endangered Species Act, the Federal Power Act and the Clean Water Act. Key pieces, as described by [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/senate-passes-sweeping-bill-simplifying-permitting-rules-build-america-rcna610196), [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/11/19/sweeping-permitting-overhaul-passes-senate-on-to-house) and the White House [Statement of Administration Policy](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/SAP-S5653.pdf):

- **Faster reviews.** Tighter deadlines for environmental reviews and fewer chokepoints for approving projects.
- **Data centers pay their way.** New data centers of 20 megawatts or more would have to pay the full added cost of the infrastructure built to serve them, according to the Bipartisan Policy Center as cited by NBC News.
- **Transmission.** New authority for the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission over interstate transmission lines, and a framework for sharing transmission costs.
- **Permit certainty.** Limits on the government's ability to revoke or reopen permits already issued, plus legal recourse for developers when an administration discriminates against certain types of energy. Roll Call notes that part is meant in part to answer the Trump administration's moves to delay or block wind and solar projects.

The White House backs it. In a [Statement of Administration Policy](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/SAP-S5653.pdf) dated Nov. 9, the Office of Management and Budget said the administration "strongly supports" the bill and that the president's advisors would recommend he sign it.

## What supporters say

"Tonight, the passage of permitting reform in the Senate finally became reality and we took a major step towards addressing the affordability concerns of the American people," Capito said in a [committee statement](https://www.epw.senate.gov/press/rep/senate-passes-capitos-bipartisan-american-affordability-and-jobs-act/).

Whitehouse said, in a statement released by [Sen. John Hickenlooper's office](https://www.hickenlooper.senate.gov/press_releases/hickenlooper-welcomes-senate-passage-of-much-needed-permitting-reform-bill/) and also quoted by [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/20/g-s1-154579/senate-bipartisan-energy-bill): "Americans will quickly begin getting some relief on their energy bills if the House follows suit."

Heinrich, in a [statement from the Energy and Natural Resources Committee's Democrats](https://www.energy.senate.gov/press/dem/heinrich-senate-passage-of-landmark-bipartisan-permitting-deal-will-lower-electricity-bills-hold-data-centers-accountable-create-over-one-hundred-thousand-jobs-reduce-carbon-emissions/), said, "We don't have to wait another year to deliver relief. We can act right now." His office cited outside analyses projecting that the bill would cut electricity prices by 3% to 5%, have data centers put about $55 billion toward transmission costs over the next decade and support up to 105,600 jobs through 2040. Those are sponsor-cited projections, not official government estimates.

Senate Majority Leader John Thune said the bill would "cut through decades of red tape," [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/20/g-s1-154579/senate-bipartisan-energy-bill) reported.

## What opponents say

Most Senate Democrats voted no. "It opens the gates to a flood of natural gas for data centers while leaving expanded transmission of renewable energy subject to Trump's obstruction," Sen. Jeff Merkley of Oregon said on X, as quoted by [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/20/g-s1-154579/senate-bipartisan-energy-bill). Sen. Maria Cantwell of Washington wrote, "Don't sacrifice the Clean Water Act, hunting or fishing grounds, historic or sacred sites, or protections of tribal sovereignty," and Sen. Alex Padilla of California said he supports cutting red tape "but not at the expense of tribal sovereignty and California's environment."

Environmental groups including the Sierra Club, the Natural Resources Defense Council and the National Wildlife Federation oppose the bill, according to [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/11/19/sweeping-permitting-overhaul-passes-senate-on-to-house) and [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/20/g-s1-154579/senate-bipartisan-energy-bill). Roll Call also reported opposition from a coalition of 17 Democratic-led states and from some tribal organizations, including the National Congress of American Indians.

No more amendments got votes. [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/11/19/sweeping-permitting-overhaul-passes-senate-on-to-house) reported that Merkley and Sen. Ron Wyden of Oregon would not agree to a time agreement for amendments, so a sponsors' package of changes, including tighter limits on data center exemptions, was left out of the bill that passed.

## What happens next

The House has to pass the same text before the bill can go to President Donald Trump. [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/11/19/sweeping-permitting-overhaul-passes-senate-on-to-house) reports that a House vote is expected after the Thanksgiving recess, and that Speaker Mike Johnson said last week the bill can pass there. [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/19/senate-passes-landmark-permitting-deal-01177557) notes that Republican leaders hope to finish it in the lame-duck session, before Democrats take control of both chambers in January.

House Majority Leader Steve Scalise said, as quoted by [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/senate-passes-sweeping-bill-simplifying-permitting-rules-build-america-rcna610196): "We want to get a bill to the president's desk, and that's still got a great chance to happen." Thune said the clearest path is for the House to pass the Senate version without changes.

Some Democrats, including Merkley and Rep. Jared Huffman of California, want the issue left to the new Congress, [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/11/19/sweeping-permitting-overhaul-passes-senate-on-to-house) reported. Heinrich and Whitehouse have argued that any bill needs Trump's signature while he is in office, and that this one is a "floor" for what Democrats can do once they hold the majority.
