Valve's current Steam Deck compatibility report for [Cronos: The New Dawn](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2101960/) (app 2101960) rates the game Playable. [SteamRaw changelist 40923294](https://steamraw.com/changelist/40923294), an independent reading of Steam's public appinfo, records the Steam Deck compatibility field changing from Verified to Playable. SteamRaw says that changelist was seen on November 13, 2026, at 17:30 UTC (11:30 a.m. CT). SteamRaw is not affiliated with Valve.

This article treats Valve's current report as the official rating, and SteamRaw as the public record of when that rating changed.

## What the official report flags

The current report's failing or warning-level results are:

- controller glyphs that do not match the Deck device
- a default configuration that is not performant
- a game or launcher that does not exit cleanly

The same report still lists the default controller configuration as fully functional and interface text as legible. It also notes that external controllers are not supported for the primary player.

On [Steam's partner documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/steamdeck/compat), Playable means the game functions on Deck, but may require manual work from the user.

Valve has not posted a separate announcement of this rating change on the game's Steam news feed, as checked on November 16.

## Valve's new framerate-data notes, as reported by SteamDeckHQ

Separately, [SteamDeckHQ reported on November 14](https://steamdeckhq.com/news/valve-clarifies-steam-deck-average-framerate/) that Valve added explanatory notes under the average Steam Deck framerate data on store pages. This article has not independently opened those notes on a store page, so the wording below is SteamDeckHQ's account of Valve's text.

SteamDeckHQ quoted the notes as saying that "only data collected at Steam Deck OLED's (LCD's) default frame limit of 90 (60) FPS is included," that "idle time and invalid samples are filtered out," and that "multiple samples are collected from each gameplay session." It also quoted Valve as saying that "gaps in the chart (e.g., between 45 and 90 FPS) can be caused by VSync," which can "cause samples to cluster at 45 and 90 FPS even when the game's average framerate falls between them."
